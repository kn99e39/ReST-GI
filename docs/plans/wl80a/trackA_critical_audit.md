### Q1. Is the factorisation physically meaningful?

**Answer: PARTIALLY.**

What holds:
- **The outer form can represent any passive K (DERIVATION).** dρZ/(ε+ΣZ) can express any nonnegative column with sum ≤ d.
- **The inner Z is exact at R = 16 (DERIVATION).** For each pair, Z is a nonnegative 16×64 block, and every such block has nonnegative rank ≤ 16. So per-pair rank 16 is exact, and rank 8 is a compression.
- **Pair-conditioned rank is physically motivated (DERIVATION, from E0 and the diffusion estimate).** Far diffusive pairs tend toward rank 1.

What is wrong:
- **ρ is redundant.** The physical total exit is Σ_{j,o}K, which is not an independent quantity.
- **w and u are not separately identifiable.** Only their product w·u is identified.
- **The source/receiver roles are asymmetric.** The exact form E_sym is symmetric: m_i K_ij ∝ (m d)_{i,a}(m d)_{j,ō}⟨p1_i, 𝒢 p1_j⟩. That form is a DERIVATION from WL76 E0 (FACT, 36/36 packets; one frozen-rule failure was resolved only by post hoc amendment 1).

### Q2. Can the inputs distinguish physically different responses?

**Answer: for interior rest contexts yes; otherwise no in at least three constructible cases.**

Cases that fail:
- **Edge-row chord mixtures (DERIVATION; magnitude HYPOTHESIS).** They can match (d, μ, Σ) but differ in κ₃ inside the ~1 mm near field.
- **Intervening deformation along the source–receiver corridor (DERIVATION).** Endpoint inputs stay unchanged while K changes.
- **Same physical response under different labels (FACT, WL79).** First-triangle bin frames on dented poses relabel one physical response non-bijectively.

Interior relative contexts are distinguishable correctly (FACT, WL78: interior transfer 0.12).

Rest or material coordinates carry identity only, not physics. For a homogeneous index-matched medium, K is a functional of the current geometry, the ports and the frame convention (DERIVATION).

### Q3. Are first-flight statistics sufficient?

**Answer: NO as physical sufficient statistics.**

- **What is actually sufficient (DERIVATION).** E0 needs the whole function p1, and E_sym needs it on both the source side and the receiver side. A1 encodes only the source side.
- **Second-order closure errors for the single-scatter depth factor (DERIVATION, re-derived by the checker).** These hold for a face-on semi-infinite medium.

  | μ_i / μ_o | Closure error |
  |---|---|
  | 1 | +10.4 % |
  | 2 | +21.8 % |
  | 4 | −17.6 % |

  - The physics specialist's figure for a cumulant closure (+21 % at r = 1, +200 % at r = 2) is a separate claim that was not re-derived.
  - Adversary 1 attributed "+200 %" to the second-order closure. The checker identified that attribution as an error, and it is not carried here.
- **Weight of single scatter.** It carries 25–36 % of collided energy (FACT, WL76 post hoc diagnostic, 16 sources). A specialist reported 16–36 % across WL74A/76.
- **Redundancy.** Collision probability equals d = 1−β (FACT, WL76).
- **Where moments are adequate.** At coarse resolution (FACT, WL74A: the span of x, y, r² holds 94.5–98.8 % of the centred incoming dependence). At port or native resolution they are not adequate (rank 6–8).

### Q4. Does pair-conditioned low rank remove the historical angular bottleneck?

**Answer: it removes the representational restriction, not the transfer bottleneck.**

What it removes (INFERENCE):
- the ATO restrictions "T has no incoming index", shared span and global outgoing hull;
- the FIA ring-lobe fit floor (0.45, FACT WL76);
- the rest orientation non-closure (FACT WL79/80).

Why the transfer bottleneck remains:
- The record puts the transfer bottleneck in the spatial geometry→T map (FACT: WL75 l.308; WL68 correlation ≈ 0 at unseen sources).
- **Bound (DERIVATION from FACT).** A perfect angular family can remove at most about 0.159 of the roughly 0.80 BND4_FAR error.
- A3 moves the angular factors into the same map that failed.

### Q5. Is the normalisation physically appropriate?

**Answer: the direction is correct; the bound, measure, domain and partition are wrong or unspecified.**

- **Correct direction (DERIVATION).** Per-input normalisation bounds the ℓ1-induced power norm. With a passive C the coupled solve then converges, which answers the WL41/42 and WL50 divergences (FACT, AGENTS.md).
- **The bound should be ω_c·d, not d (DERIVATION).** WL76's Q already used Σ Q ≤ albedo (FACT).
- **Measure and domain are missing (INFERENCE).** There is no port-area × projected-solid-angle weight, which is wrong under deformation, and no restriction to the optical domain.
- **No single local/far budget (DERIVATION from FACT).** Without one, either passivity fails (up to 2dρ) or the far energy is pushed into the near field (about +5.5 % red for interior sources).

### Q6. Does the normalisation couple unrelated receivers?

**Answer: YES, structurally (DERIVATION).**

- An edit outside the pair contexts rescales every near receiver and every bin by the common factor (ρ′/ρ)(S/S′).
- Physically the change is δK_ij ∝ ψ_i(x_E)ψ†_j(x_E): it depends on the receiver and is near 0 off the corridor.
- Illustrative magnitude: about −5 % uniformly where physics gives about 0 (SPECULATION).
- Removing the artefact forces ρ ∝ S/(m·d), which is the same condition exact reciprocity imposes. A free ρ is therefore incompatible with both locality and reciprocity.

### Q7. Is boundary context sufficient for nonlocal transport?

**Answer: endpoint context plus displacement is provably insufficient (DERIVATION).**

- **Counterexample (diffusion approximation; magnitude HYPOTHESIS).** A 4 mm underside dent midway along a 12 mm pair changes red transfer by about e^{−1.2} ≈ 0.3×. Every endpoint input stays the same.
- **Screening lengths (DERIVATION).** 1/e lengths are about 3.5 / 3.1 / 1.6 mm for R/G/B, set mostly by thickness (H_eff ≈ 11.3 mm). A single context radius is therefore mis-scaled for at least one channel.
- **Corridor width (INFERENCE).** The proposed corridor width sqrt(r·ℓ) is unjustified. In this weakly absorbing slab the diffusive band fills the thickness within a few mm (checker).

### Q8. Can source identity still leak?

**Answer: YES, through at least six routes.**

1. Index-ordered k-NN ties: 1,114–5,852 edges per operation (FACT, WL80).
2. Unclipped boundary distances, an affine bijection of position up to D4h (DERIVATION).
3. Face-dependent t/b frame convention (FACT, WL74A).
4. First-triangle frames on dents (FACT, WL79).
5. Per-geometry fitted pair latents (DERIVATION). The proposal does not say whether latents are encoded or fitted.
6. A receiver set in the denominator that depends on tessellation (DERIVATION). Absolute far-cell ids are a further possible route (HYPOTHESIS).

Removing explicit keys has already been tried. In WL78 it fixed only the interior; the equivalent-context difference Q78/A75 was 1.00 / 1.03 (FACT).

### Q9. Is angular equivariance guaranteed?

**Answer: only for the rest D4h group.**

- Rest maps are exact (FACT, WL79; bin residual ≤ 6e-16).
- Averaging is an orthogonal projection, so it cannot increase L2 error against an equivariant truth (DERIVATION).
- **Not covered:**
  - continuous in-plane SO(2): it is not a permutation of the 8×8 concentric grid (DERIVATION);
  - the per-port frame gauge, e.g. a 45° change after re-meshing (DERIVATION);
  - deformed poses (FACT, WL79: residual 0.06–0.16, non-bijective outgoing maps).
- **Budget caveat.** If d is noisy (β noise 0.58–0.60 %, FACT WL79), averaging can push a column above its own budget.
- **Expressivity tension (FACT, WL76/77).** Invariant features alone gave the ring-lobe floor, so A3 would need to output frame-covariant factors.

### Q10. Behaviour under deformation?

**Answer: no guarantees.**

- The A5 averaging is undefined at deformed poses (FACT, WL79).
- A moment code learned at rest can mis-decode changed chord families (INFERENCE).
- The Q6 coupling artefact appears under every edit outside the pair contexts (DERIVATION).
- The area measure changes under deformation (INFERENCE).
- Bending changes K globally and at high rank (FACT, AGENTS.md WL53 summary). Material-frame displacement hides the world-space chord changes that drive this (INFERENCE).
- The σ_t density law under deformation is unspecified (INFERENCE).
- Historical learned models failed DEF (FACT, WL76/78). WL78 was worse than A75 on DEF.

### Q11. Is the operator identifiable from sparse source labels?

**Answer: out-of-orbit boundary contexts are not identified.**

- **Coverage.** S16-R has 12 interior sources and 4 boundary orbits at 1.8–4.0 mm (FACT, WL73/79). Under relative inputs the 12 interior sources collapse to about one context (DERIVATION).
- **Gauges.** The w·u scale and the Z scale are benign gauges. The ε / ρ trade-off is harmful.
- **The budget is confounded.** A Monte Carlo d is off by up to 0.075 absolute (FACT, WL68/WL74A).
- **Reciprocity is unused.** It is the only exact constraint across columns, and it is excluded by the independent ρ.
  - It pins entries K(i→j) where j is a supervised position (or an orbit image of one). This is a correction by the checker to Adversary 2, who said it pins none.
  - Those pinned entries lie mainly in the far field, so the near-field freedom survives.
- **BGC is less identified than ATO (INFERENCE).** Pair-conditioned angular factors add unknowns per pair, whereas ATO's global A/O was identified jointly by all sources.

### Q12. Memory and inference cost?

**Answer: feasible only in a narrow regime, and not compressive at the rank the evidence points to (DERIVATION; timings INFERENCE).**

Storage:
- Factors need 243R floats per pair, against 3,072 for the dense block. Crossover R* = 12.64.

| Configuration (fp32) | Size |
|---|---|
| N128 R4 | 1.91 GB |
| N128 R8 | 3.82 GB |
| N128 R16 | 7.64 GB |
| N512 R8 | 15.3 GB |
| N512 R16 | 30.6 GB |
| Dense local block, N128 / N512 | 6.04 / 24.2 GB |

Apply:
- About 81× a global ATO per pair, because a pair-dependent v cannot be factored out of the source sum.
- A naive einsum would need about 24 GB of intermediates. The WL76 dense scatter took 3.07 s; CSR brought it to 0.25–0.35 s (FACT).

Far field and decode:
- Red far share outside N128 is 5.5 % for interior sources (FACT, WL78) and about 6–22 % by diffusion estimate (DERIVATION).
- Latent-only decode costs about 15–30 ms per K application at N128 R8 and 0.1–0.2 s at N512 R16 (INFERENCE). Each solve needs 6–20 applications.

A5 averaging multiplies generator cost by 16 and makes the averaged blocks generically rank 16.

Any cost claim is subject to the mandatory baseline gate in `docs/BASELINE_ROLES_AND_EVIDENCE_STRATEGY.md`.

### Q13. Is BGC meaningfully different from existing methods?

**Answer: NOT as a mechanism (INFERENCE).** See the novelty assessment. Every element maps to prior art or project history. A3+A4 is a hypernetwork generalisation of ATO.

### Q14. Does BGC avoid the failure mechanism of RSK (WL72 B)?

**Answer: not for the dominant part.**

- RSK's failure mechanism was never attributed (FACT, WL72).
- A2 is close to RSK / W+UV-Q (INFERENCE).
- BGC removes two confounds RSK's evaluation had: the fixed-A/O boundary fit floor and the rest orbit mismatch.
  - WL75 removed the angular floor and far transfer did not change (FACT).
  - The failing contexts (side face s46, corners s43/s44, UNS, TBE-far) are out of orbit.
- **Late-training specialisation recurs (FACT).** The @2000 checkpoint beats the final one in WL72/73/75, and Q78 rose from 0.63 to 0.86. More per-pair capacity likely amplifies this (INFERENCE).

### Q15. Strongest counterexample

**Orbit-consistent bump (DERIVATION).** Set w′ = w(1+λφψ):
- φ is orbit-invariant and vanishes on all supervised contexts;
- ψ is non-uniform over receivers.

The result keeps every hard property and every training label, and it changes held-out boundary near-field contrast by O(1).

Checker caveat: the bump construction applies to any finitely supervised learned operator. What makes it count against BGC is that BGC also lacks a structural mechanism for sharing information across sources, and that its only new element targets a factor the record calls non-binding.

See the counterexamples section for the full list.

### CE-1. Orbit-consistent bump: identification (DERIVATION)
**Sources:** representation, Adv1 O4, Adv2, novelty.

**Construction.**
- Take any BGC instance θ that fits S16-R.
- Let φ ≥ 0 be a smooth bump on orbit-invariant source-context descriptors. It vanishes on every interior context and on the D4h orbits of TBE, TBC, SM and SR. It is nonzero, for example, on UNS or at 8 mm from an edge.
- Let ψ be a non-uniform weighting over receivers or mixtures. It must be non-uniform, because a uniform bump cancels in the normalisation.
- Set w′ = w(1+λφψ).

**What θ′ still satisfies.**
- It is linear, positive and passive.
- It is exactly D4h-equivariant.
- It contains no identity key.
- Its training loss is identical to θ's.

**What changes.** Held-out boundary near-field contrast changes by O(1). That near field holds 99.0–99.9 % of the native signal (FACT, WL78 D1). It is also where WL72, WL75 and WL78 failed (FACT).

**Qualifications.**
- *Reciprocity, corrected by the checker.* It pins entries toward supervised positions, mostly in the far field. So the near-field freedom survives; it is not true that "no entry" is pinned.
- *Scope, from the checker.* The construction is generic to finitely supervised learned operators. It discriminates against BGC only together with BGC's lack of any structural mechanism that shares information across sources.

### CE-2. Normalisation–locality conflict (DERIVATION; magnitude SPECULATION)
**Source:** physics Q15.

**Setup.**
- Source i on the top face. Near receivers lie within about 3 mm.
- A thinning dent sits 8–12 mm away, inside the ω = 0.999 range (λ ≈ 3.5 mm) but outside N_i and outside the near corridors.

**Physics.** The near-receiver change is about exp(−excess/λ), roughly 1 % (DERIVATION).

**BGC with a source-local ρ.** Every near receiver, in every bin, is scaled by S/S′.
- Hypothetical numbers: near 0.60, tail 0.30 → 0.35.
- Then K_near = 0.9·0.60/0.95 = 0.568, about −5 % uniformly.

**Consequence.** Any fix forces ρ ∝ S/(m·d), which is the same condition reciprocity needs. A free ρ is therefore incompatible with both locality and reciprocity.

### CE-3. Single-scatter moment closure (DERIVATION; re-derived by the checker)
**Sources:** physics Q3, Adv1 O7.

**Exact result.** For a face-on semi-infinite medium, the single-scatter depth factor is E[e^{−z/(ℓμ_o)}] = μ_o/(μ_i+μ_o).

**Second-order Taylor closure errors.**

| μ_i/μ_o | Error |
|---|---|
| 1 | +10.4 % |
| 2 | +21.8 % |
| 4 | −17.6 % |

**Weight.** Single scatter carries 25–36 % of collided energy (FACT, WL76 post hoc diagnostic).

**Rebuttal and reply.** A learned decoder can invert the moments when p1 belongs to a known family. The learned inversion is then invalid when the family changes: tilted, curved or dented ports, or truncated or bimodal chords (INFERENCE).

### CE-4. Edge-row chord mixture (DERIVATION; magnitude HYPOTHESIS)
**Source:** representation Q2.

**Construction.**
- A grazing source at 1.25 mm from a side face gives a mixture of truncated exponentials with chord lengths of about 0 to several mm.
- A single-chord configuration matches all 10 scalars (d, μ, Σ) but not κ₃.

**Consequence.** Side-face receivers fed within about 1 mm respond differently, so A1 cannot distinguish them.

### CE-5. Midway underside dent (DERIVATION, diffusion approximation; magnitude HYPOTHESIS)
**Source:** representation Q7.

**Construction.**
- i and j are 12 mm apart.
- A midway underside dent thins the slab to 4 mm. Both endpoints are at least 5 mm from the rim.

**Result.**
- Every endpoint input is unchanged: contexts, displacement, frames and thicknesses.
- Red transfer changes by about e^{−1.2} ≈ 0.3× (slab-mode constant 0.28 → 0.59/mm).

**Consequence.** Endpoint context is provably insufficient.

### CE-6. Centre vs near-side-face port (DERIVATION)
**Source:** physics Q3.

**Construction.** Normal incidence at a top-face port at the face centre, and at one 2 mm from a side face.

**Result.**
- The relative (d, μ, Σ) are identical.
- K differs, because light escapes through the side face.

**Consequence.** Sufficiency can only be judged for A1 and A2 jointly, and A2 is underspecified.

### CE-7. 45° per-port frame gauge (DERIVATION)
**Source:** physics Q9.

**Construction.** Re-mesh a port so that its first-triangle tangent rotates by 45°.

**Result.**
- K is physically identical.
- The bin values cannot be transformed exactly, because a 45° rotation is not a D4 permutation of the 8×8 grid.
- Global D4h averaging does not cover this gauge.

### CE-8. Systems rank/cache counterexample (DERIVATION; timings INFERENCE)
**Source:** systems Q15.

**Storage.**
- If the needed per-pair rank is 13 or more, the factors exceed the dense block, and BGC becomes a neural generator of a dense local K.

**Support size.**
- At N128, roughly 6–22 % of red flux lies outside the local support (diffusion estimate). WL78 measured 5.5 % for interior sources (FACT). The far part must therefore be accurate.
- At N512 R16 the cache is 30.6 GB fp32, which exceeds both 12 GB and 16 GB GPUs.

**Latent-only alternative.** About 0.7–4 s per frame.

**Escape condition (HYPOTHESIS).** The counterexample does not apply if per-pair nonnegative rank ≤ 4 suffices for near and edge pairs; N128 R4 would then need 1.9 GB. This quantity is unmeasured.

### CE-9. Reflected-lobe twin (DERIVATION; locality assumption unchecked)
**Source:** novelty Q15.

**Construction.**
- K2 equals K1 except on the columns of the orbit of an out-of-orbit boundary source s*.
- On those columns, for receivers within 5 mm only, each outgoing distribution is reflected about the in-face axis perpendicular to the edge, conjugated consistently across the orbit.

**What K2 still satisfies.**
- Positive and passive, with the same per-receiver mass.
- D4h-equivariant.
- Matches every label.

**What changes.** Near-field contrast. The construction assumes no training port lies within 5 mm of s*, which was not checked against every population.

Scope: I only read the repository: AGENTS.md and worklogs 76, 77, 77A, 79 and 80. I opened nothing under `docs/plans/wl80a/`, wrote no files and took no new measurements. The numbers in derivations are my own analytic estimates, not measurements.

## Common basis for all questions

**Notation.**
- m(i,a) = port area A_i × projected solid angle of bin a.
- K is a power fraction.
- p1(·|i,a) is the normalised first-collision density.
- ℓ = 1 mm, σ_t = 1/ℓ, isotropic phase (g = 0).

**E0 (FACT, WL76 Stage 1).** K(i,a→j,o) = d(i,a) · E_{x∼p1(·|i,a)}[Q(x→j,o)].
- Q does not depend on the source (Markov property plus isotropic phase).
- The decomposition matched the archived references in 36/36 packets, and the forward control was rejected 4/4.
- One frozen-rule failure (single-scatter self-consistency, z² 1.70) was resolved only by post hoc amendment 1.

**E1 (DERIVATION).** By reciprocity of uncollided flight, the escape probability from an interior point y into (j,o) is m(j,o)·d(j,ō)·p1(y|j,ō)/(4πσ_t), where ō is the reversed direction. Substituting into E0:

m(i,a) K(i,a→j,o) = (ω/4πσ_t) · [m(i,a)d(i,a)] · [m(j,o)d(j,ō)] · ⟨p1(·|i,a), 𝒢 p1(·|j,ō)⟩

- 𝒢 = δ + ωT + (ωT)² + …, where T(x,y) = σ_t e^{−σ_t r}/(4πr²) is the symmetric next-collision kernel.
- The exact operator is therefore a symmetric bilinear form of two first-collision densities: one for the source beam and one for the reversed receiver beam.
- This agrees with the one-line WL77A record "g·K = 4πσω⟨P, G P⟩". I did not inspect that worklog's own derivation.

**E2 (DERIVATION, diffusion approximation; only rough for ω = 0.9 and within a few ℓ of the source).**
- Diffusion length L_d = ℓ/√(3(1−ω)): 18.3 / 5.8 / 1.8 mm for ω = 0.999 / 0.99 / 0.9.
- The index-matched faces leak, so lateral decay inside the slab has length 1/√(L_d⁻² + (π/T_eff)²), with T_eff ≈ 10 + 4ℓ/3 ≈ 11.3 mm.
- That gives about 3.5 / 3.1 / 1.6 mm per channel. Lateral reach is set mostly by the slab thickness, not by the albedo.

## Q1. Is the factorisation physically meaningful?

**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** It can represent the operator, but it is not physically structured, and two of its terms are physically redundant or wrong.

**What holds.**
- **Outer d·ρ·Z/ΣZ (DERIVATION).** Any nonnegative row with sum ≤ d can be written this way, so it is an exact reparametrisation.
- **Inner Z (DERIVATION).** Per pair (i,j), Z is a nonnegative mixture over a 16 × 64 (a,o) block. Its nonnegative rank is ≤ 16, so per-pair rank 16 is exact in principle and rank 8 is a compression.
- **Rank should fall with distance (DERIVATION from E1 and E2).** Far from the source the collision density near the receiver is diffusive, so K_ij(a,o) → f_i(a)·g_j(o), which is rank 1. Pair-conditioned rank is therefore physically better motivated than a global ATO basis.
  - WL77A records pair angular blocks of rank about 4 as a specialist observation (non-evidence, point statistics).
  - WL79 found that the global A/O factors are not closed under D4 (relative assignment error 0.46–0.62, FACT). A pair-conditioned model removes that particular obstruction (INFERENCE).

**What fails.**
- **ρ is redundant (DERIVATION).** In E0 and E1 the total escape is exactly Σ_{j,o} K. No independent "escape fraction" exists. BGC predicts ρ separately from ΣZ, so ρ and ΣZ carry the same physical quantity twice and can disagree (see Q6).
- **Reciprocity is not built in (DERIVATION).** E1 implies m_i d_i ρ_i Z_ij/S_i = m_j d_j ρ_j Z_ji/S_j, where S_i = ΣZ. Even with a symmetric Z this requires ρ_i = c·S_i/(m_i d_i) for every connected pair. So ρ must be a fixed function of the global row sum.
  - Any instance with an independently predicted ρ is generically non-reciprocal.
  - According to AGENTS.md's summary of WL53, the oracle K was reciprocal (0.998–1.000).
  - Reciprocity is exactly checkable here. Direction reversal is a 180° in-plane rotation, which is a D4 element, so it is an exact bin permutation. The 8 × 8 → 4 × 4 aggregation nests (INFERENCE from the bin map described in WL79).
- **u and v are asymmetric (DERIVATION).** E1 treats source and receiver identically: both are first-collision densities. BGC uses a conditional incoming response u and a normalised outgoing distribution v instead. The roles of w, u and v are not identifiable: the w·u scale and the ΣZ scale are gauge freedoms (INFERENCE: a learning concern, not a validity concern).

## Q3. Are first-flight statistics sufficient?

**Verdict: NO** as physical sufficient statistics. Whether they are an adequate approximation is untested.

**Exact sufficient statistics (DERIVATION from E0 and E1).**
- Source side: the whole function d·p1(·|i,a).
- Receiver side: the whole function d·p1(·|j,ō). A1 encodes only the source side, but E1 needs the same kind of object at the receiver.
- Geometry inside the transport range (E2).

**Hand-built closure counterexample: single scatter (DERIVATION).**
- Single scatter is the δ part of 𝒢, an overlap integral of two exponential beams.
- For a face-on semi-infinite region the depth factor is exact: E[e^{−z/(ℓμ_o)}] with z ∼ Exp(ℓμ_i) equals μ_o/(μ_i+μ_o).
- A second-order Taylor closure about the mean gives e^{−r}(1+r²/2), with r = μ_i/μ_o.

| r = μ_i/μ_o | Second-order closure error |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | +2 % |
| 1 | +10 % |
| 2 | +22 % |
| 4 | −18 % |

- A cumulant closure is worse: +21 % at r = 1 and +200 % at r = 2.
- Single scatter carries 25–36 % of the reference collided energy (WL76 post hoc diagnostic on F76_7601, 16 sources: FACT for that diagnostic).

**Rebuttal and the reply to it (INFERENCE).** A network can invert (d, μ, Σ) back to p1 when p1 belongs to a known parametric family. At rest that family is bin- and port-averaged truncated exponentials along rays. If the network does this, the moments work as a code for the family, not as physical statistics. The inversion learned at rest is invalid when the family changes:
- curved or tilted ports;
- truncated or bimodal chords near edges or dents.

**Relative statistics without boundary context are insufficient (DERIVATION).** Take normal incidence at a top-face port at the centre of the face and at one 2 mm from a side face. The relative (d, μ, Σ) are identical, but K differs: light escapes through the side face. Sufficiency can only be judged for A1 and A2 together, and A2 is not specified concretely enough to judge.

**Historical context.**
- Incident-side geometry is the part that has worked. WL76's exact geometric p1 quadrature got the per-incoming-bin energy profile right (relative L2 0.023, FACT).
- The deployable estimates were noise-limited: q = 1024 single-scatter noise was 0.45–0.48 of the multiple-scatter norm (WL77A, coordinator check, non-evidence). If A1's statistics come from Monte Carlo quadrature, they inherit this noise (INFERENCE).

## Q5. Is the normalisation physically appropriate?

**Verdict: correct direction, wrong bound, unspecified measure.**

**Correct direction (DERIVATION).** Normalising per input (i,a) over outputs bounds the ℓ1-induced norm, which is the maximum column sum in power units. With a passive C this gives ‖KC‖₁ < 1 and therefore a convergent coupled solve. That directly addresses the historical divergences recorded in AGENTS.md: WL41/42 non-passivity, WL50 ρ 7–9.

**Bound is too loose (DERIVATION).** A first collision absorbs with probability 1−ω, so Σ K ≤ ω(c)·d. WL76's Q already used ΣQ ≤ albedo (FACT). A ρ bounded by 1 instead of ω(c):
- allows non-physical escape for ω = 0.9;
- lets ‖K‖ approach 1 at d ≈ 1, giving slow or marginal convergence in self-facing configurations (INFERENCE).

**Measure is unspecified (INFERENCE).** ΣZ is energy only if each Z(j,o) carries port area × bin measure.
- At rest, ports are equal 2.5 mm squares. I infer the bins have equal projected solid angle because the concentric map is equal-area; I have not verified this.
- Under deformation, port areas change. A density-like network output summed without area weights mis-distributes power.

**Normalisation domain is unspecified (INFERENCE).**
- It must be restricted to the same optical domain (FNTO ownership). Strictly positive activations give non-zero K at unreachable receivers.
- A local-plus-tail split (A6) needs a second predicted global share τ. WL76 FIA had exactly this τ (FACT), and it has the same consistency problem as ρ.

**Linearity holds (INFERENCE).** Z depends only on geometry, so K stays linear in the incident field. This requires that A1/A6 features are computed per basis input (i,a) and never aggregated over the actual illumination before the network.

## Q6. Does the normalisation couple unrelated receivers?

**Verdict: YES, structurally (DERIVATION).**

∂K(j,o)/∂Z(j',o') = −dρ·Z(j,o)/S² for every (j',o') ≠ (j,o).

Suppose Z(i,j) depends only on geometry in N_i ∪ N_j ∪ the corridor between them, and ρ depends only on N_i. Then an edit E outside those regions changes K(i,j,·,·) for every such j by one common factor, (ρ'/ρ)(S/S'), the same for every receiver and every bin.

**Exact physics (DERIVATION, first-order perturbation theory).** δK_ij ∝ ψ_i(x_E)·ψ†_j(x_E), the forward fluence from the source times the adjoint importance for the receiver, both evaluated at the edit. The relative change is therefore of order exp(−(r_iE + r_Ej − r_ij)/λ), with λ from E2.
- It depends on the receiver.
- It goes to 0 for receivers whose corridor misses E. BGC instead gives them a common non-zero factor of about −ΔS/S.
- The error cancels only if ρ' = ρ·S'/S exactly, which makes ρ redundant (Q1).

**Physical competition is different (INFERENCE).** For ω = 0.999 the slab is nearly conservative, so physical receivers do compete for photons. But the competition is localised: extra escape at an edit is drawn mostly from long-path photons that would otherwise have been absorbed or would have exited along routes passing the edit. It is not a proportional drain on the near field.

## Q9. Is angular equivariance guaranteed?

**Verdict: only for the rest D4h group, and only through averaging.** Not for the symmetries that matter for transfer.

**What the averaging gives.**
- **Exact at rest (FACT, WL79).** The rest port and bin maps are exact bijections (bin-centre residual ≤ 6e-16), including the reflections.
- **Never increases error at rest (DERIVATION).** Averaging is an orthogonal projection when the group acts by permutations, so it cannot increase L2 error against an equivariant truth. It also preserves positivity and row normalisation.

**Caveats.**
- **Budget noise (INFERENCE).** If d comes from quadrature, d is G-invariant only up to the 0.58–0.60 % relative β noise measured in WL79 (FACT). The averaged row sum can then exceed that row's own d(i,a) when ρ is close to ω. The budget must be averaged too.
- **Continuous symmetry is not representable (DERIVATION).** Away from the side faces, the physical local symmetry is SO(2) rotation about the normal plus translation. The source–receiver azimuth φ_r is continuous, but the 8 × 8 concentric bin grid is invariant only under D4. A rotation not in D4 is not a bin permutation, and resampling is not invertible. Exact equivariance therefore holds on 8 of the continuum of in-plane rotations; everything else must be learned.
- **Equivariance–expressivity tension (FACT, WL76 and WL77).**
  - WL76 FIA got exact equivariance from invariant features, but only with azimuth-uniform ring lobes, which caused its angular floor.
  - WL77 needed the relative azimuth φ_o − φ_r to escape that floor.
  - The ring floor was 0.45 at q = 1024; WL77A extrapolates about 0.21–0.22 noise-free (coordinator check, non-evidence).
  - A3 must output frame-covariant factors; invariant features alone cannot produce them.
- **Local frame gauge is not covered (DERIVATION).** A port whose first-triangle tangent is rotated by 45° (for example after remeshing) has physically identical K, but the bin values cannot be transformed exactly. Global D4h averaging does not address this local gauge.

## Q10. Behaviour under deformation

**Verdict: no guarantees.** Several mechanisms degrade.

- **No group exists (FACT, WL79).** At dented poses the normals tilt by up to 0.073–0.079, the bin residual is 0.06–0.16, and the outgoing maps are not bijective. The A5 averaging is undefined there. Local physical symmetries still hold, but nothing enforces them.
- **A1 stays exact only if computed exactly (INFERENCE).** First-flight terms are recomputable from chords, but:
  - a moment code learned at rest mis-decodes changed families (Q3);
  - quadrature recomputation is costly and noisy (WL76 FACT: rebuild 75.0 s, of which 74.3 s quadrature; WL77A noise as in Q3).
- **Normalisation under deformation.** The Q6 uniform-factor artefact appears for every edit outside the pair contexts, and the area measure changes (Q5). Contexts must reach about 3–5 decay lengths (E2), roughly 10–15 mm for ω = 0.999, and must cover the i–j corridor.
- **Bending is global (AGENTS.md summary of WL53).** Bend changes K globally and at high rank, while dent changes are local and low-rank. Any material-frame relative displacement hides the world-space chord changes that the transport actually sees (INFERENCE).
- **Medium density under deformation is undefined (INFERENCE).** The proposal does not say whether σ_t is constant in world space or mass-conserving (scaled by the inverse volume ratio). The two give different physics.
- **Track record (FACT, WL76).** The historical learned models failed DEF71/DEF73 (abs_coarse ≥ 1.3 in WL76). Nothing in BGC addresses the mechanisms behind that.

## Q15. Strongest counterexample from my angle

**Normalisation–locality incompatibility (DERIVATION; magnitude SPECULATION).**

**Setup.**
- Source i on the top face. Receivers j within about 3 mm of i.
- Edit E: a thinning dent about 8–12 mm away, inside the ω = 0.999 transport range (λ ≈ 3.5 mm) but outside N_i and outside the near receivers' corridors.

**Exact physics.**
- The relative change at the near receivers is about exp(−excess path/λ), an excess of roughly 13–16 mm, so of order 1 %.
- Extra escape at the dent comes from long-path photons that would otherwise have been absorbed.
- Total escape rises.

**BGC with source-local ρ.**
- All near receivers, in every bin, are scaled by S/S'.
- Hypothetical numbers: near 0.60, tail 0.30 → 0.35. Then K_near = 0.9·0.60/0.95 = 0.568, about −5 % uniformly, where the physical change is about 0.
- Any fix forces ρ ∝ S. The same condition is required for reciprocity, so a separately predicted ρ is incompatible with both locality and reciprocity at once.

**Secondary counterexamples.**
- The single-scatter moment closure (Q3: +10 % to +200 % depth-factor errors at μ_i ≥ μ_o).
- The 45° frame gauge (Q9).

## Screening-length derivation (DERIVATION; diffusion approximation, crude within about 1 mfp of a boundary)

- **Inputs.** σ_t = 1/mm, D ≈ 1/3 mm, extrapolation length z_b ≈ 2D ≈ 0.67 mm (index-matched). The effective thickness is H_e ≈ 11.3 mm, so the lowest slab mode is k = π/H_e ≈ 0.28/mm.
- **Lateral decay** κ = √(k² + σ_a/D):
  - R (σ_a = 0.001): κ = 0.283/mm, 1/e length about 3.5 mm;
  - G (σ_a = 0.01): κ = 0.327/mm, about 3.1 mm;
  - B (σ_a = 0.1): κ = 0.614/mm, about 1.6 mm.
- **Consequence.** In this fixture the pair response is screened within roughly 5–15 mm, and the required screening radius differs by about 2× between channels. This agrees with H3 and H15.

## Q2. Can the inputs distinguish physically different responses?

**Verdict.** Yes for interior rest contexts. No in three constructible cases. The reverse failure, where identical physics produces different inputs, also occurs (see Q8).

**(a) A1 first-collision moments.**
- Exact factorisation (WL74A §4.1; I re-derived it): `Z_i[a](j,o) = ∫ p_{i,a}(x) G(x; j,o) dx`. So (collision probability, mean, covariance) is sufficient only if G is at most quadratic in x over the support of p. The leading error is about (1/6)·κ₃·∂³G.
- Near a boundary, G for exit at that boundary varies on about the 1 mm scale, which is the scale of p itself (mfp 1 mm). In blue the screening length is 1.6 mm. Single scatter carries 16–36 % of the collided energy (WL74A, WL76).
- **Counterexample (DERIVATION; magnitude HYPOTHESIS).** Take an edge-row source port (1.25 mm from a side face) with grazing incidence toward the edge.
  - Entry points spread across the 2.5 mm port, so p is a mixture of truncated exponentials whose chord lengths run from about 0 to several mm.
  - A single-chord configuration can match all 10 scalars (d, μ, Σ) but not κ₃.
  - The side-face receivers adjacent to the source are fed by collisions within about 1 mm of that face, so their responses differ.
- **Supporting fact for A1 (H5).** At coarse resolution, first- and second-order footprint terms carry 94.5–98.8 % of the incoming dependence. A1 is therefore plausible at the coarse level and suspect at native, fine16 and edge level (port-resolution rank 6–8).
- **INFERENCE.** A1 encodes only p. The post-collision G, which is where transfer failed historically, is left entirely to A2/A3.

**(b) Endpoint-only context.** Fails when the geometry between source and receiver changes (see Q7).

**(c) Deformed ports.** The bin-frame convention (first-triangle) makes the same physical response appear under non-bijective relabellings (H11, FACT). The "material-local frame" input therefore carries the convention, not physics (INFERENCE).

**(d) Interior rest.** With relative inputs, equal contexts give identical inputs and correct transfer (H10, FACT).

**(e) Rest/material coordinates (DERIVATION).** For a homogeneous isotropic index-matched medium, K is a functional of the current domain shape, the port patches and the frame convention only. Rest coordinates can never separate responses that the current geometry does not already separate; they can only separate identities.
- WL78's DEF regression after removing absolute inputs is therefore consistent with removing a shortcut rather than losing information. This is INFERENCE; WL78 left the cause unattributed.

## Q4. Does pair-conditioned low rank remove the historical angular bottleneck?

**Verdict.** It removes the representational part. It does not remove the bottleneck to transfer, which was not angular, and it moves the angular burden onto the learned geometry→operator map that already failed.

**What it removes (DERIVATION from H1, H2, H3).**
- Per pair, `Σ_r w·u_r⊗v_r` is any nonnegative rank-≤R block.
- u depends on j, so the incoming-direction-dependent lateral footprint shift becomes expressible. This removes the structural "T has no incoming index" restriction (WL74A P3), as well as the shared-span (P4/P5) and global outgoing-hull (P6) restrictions.
- Factors computed in local frames, combined with A5 averaging, remove the WL79/80 orientation non-closure at rest.
- The needed per-pair rank is about 4 (H3), and per-source rank 8 with its own A/O reaches 0.07–0.11 (H2). R = 8 per pair is therefore ample for SVD rank.
- Nonnegative rank of 8 or below is HYPOTHESIS (not measured).

**What it does not address.**
- **FACT.** H7 and H8: with fixed global A/O the oracle passes on confirmation ports where learned models fail by 2.5–6.5×. Rank 16 left far-boundary transfer unchanged.
- **DERIVATION.** Degrees of freedom per pair and channel are R(1+16+64) = 648, against 1,024 entries. Per pair the representation is barely compressive, so all regularity has to come from the encoder.
- **INFERENCE.**
  - Global A/O was a shared prior and denoiser. Outgoing shape is near-Lambertian (H4), and far-receiver 64-bin labels have low SNR.
  - Pair-conditioned v therefore adds variance more than it reduces bias.
  - Held-out angular error can get worse than with ATO, where the angular part generalised trivially.

## Q7. Is boundary context sufficient for nonlocal transport?

**Verdict.** Endpoint context plus displacement is provably insufficient. Approximate sufficiency would require a region-level descriptor covering the corridor out to a few channel-dependent screening lengths.

**Counterexample (DERIVATION; magnitude HYPOTHESIS).**
- Set i and j 12 mm apart, with an underside dent midway that thins the slab to 4 mm. Both endpoints are at least 5 mm from the dent rim.
- The endpoint contexts (radius 4 mm or less), the displacement, the frames and the endpoint thicknesses are all identical to the undeformed slab.
- The slab-mode constant rises from about 0.28 to about 0.59/mm over the thin segment. Over 4 mm that attenuates red transfer by about e^{−1.2} ≈ 0.3×, an O(1) relative change in K(i,j).
- Its absolute weight in coarse metrics is small (about e^{−12/3.5} of the near field), but the deformation-delta metric is exactly this signal.

**Why a straight-segment descriptor is not enough (INFERENCE).** If A2's undefined "pairwise geometric context" samples only the straight segment, it still misses multiply scattered paths, which sample a band of width about √(r·ℓ).

**Consequences.**
- **DERIVATION.** On this fixture the separate long-range representation (A6) carries a negligible share, and its split from the local part must be disjoint (D3).
- **DERIVATION.** A single context radius is either too small for red or over-identifying for blue.

## Q8. Can source identity still leak?

**Verdict.** Yes, through several routes. Two of them are documented in the current pipeline.

1. **Index-ordered k-NN ties (FACT, H12).** 1,114–5,852 edges per operation. Any k-based sparse pair set inherits them. Through the A4 denominator, the per-source total then also depends on the tie-break order (DERIVATION).
2. **Boundary distances as a position fingerprint (DERIVATION).** In a 100×100×10 box, the distances to the faces fix position up to D4h. Any context whose radius reaches a boundary encodes position across the whole boundary band. That band is exactly where the 4 boundary training sources sit and where transfer fails. Group averaging does not remove this, because it averages over the orbit and the fingerprint is orbit-invariant.
3. **Face-dependent frame convention (FACT, WL74A §3.1).** The thin axis is t on f0/f3 but b on f1/f2. Frame-expressed inputs therefore carry face identity. On dents, the first-triangle frames carry the triangulation index (H11).
4. **Pair latents (A6; DERIVATION).**
   - Latents fitted per geometry (auto-decoder style) are free per-pair parameters, which is a learned identity key.
   - Only latents produced by an encoder are leak-free.
   - The proposal does not say which.
5. **Long-range cells.** If they are indexed by absolute cell id, as RSK's 240-cell far relations may have been, absolute position leaks (HYPOTHESIS; the design is unspecified).
6. **Normalisation receiver set (DERIVATION).** The set of receivers in the denominator depends on tessellation and port count, which leaks mesh identity under deformation.

**Historical precedent (FACT, H10).** Removing explicit keys fixed the interior only.

## Q11. Identifiability from sparse source labels

**Verdict.** Out-of-orbit boundary contexts are not identified. Interior contexts are identified through invariance. There are also two harmful non-identifiabilities beyond the benign gauge.

**Coverage (DERIVATION, using H16).**
- Under relative inputs, the 12 interior sources collapse to about one context.
- Boundary-context space has at least 3 parameters (distance to the first side face, distance to a second side face, height on a side face) plus deformation. It is sampled at 4 orbits.
- The bump construction in Q15 leaves every label unchanged.
- Pair-conditioned angular factors add unknowns per pair. That makes BGC strictly less identified than ATO, whose global A/O was identified jointly by all sources.

**Gauge.**
- Rescaling or permuting (w, u, v) over r is benign.
- A uniform scaling of Z per (i,a) cancels in the normalisation, which is benign. It does mean any regulariser on Z is unanchored.
- If ε is not much smaller than ΣZ, ρ and |Z| trade off, and ρ stops being the "total outgoing fraction". This is harmful.

**Budget d (FACT H14 plus DERIVATION).**
- If d_model < d_true, ρ ≤ 1 cannot reach the targets.
- If d_model > d_true, the passivity bound is physically wrong.
- The identification of ρ is confounded with quadrature error of up to 0.075 absolute.

**Reciprocity is unused.**
- FACT: the reference K is reciprocal (H17).
- DERIVATION: a labelled source with dense receivers implies labels for every port acting as a source toward that labelled port, at 16-bin resolution after nesting the 64 outgoing bins into 16. Exact bin-reversal correspondence is INFERENCE.
- The per-source normalisation `K = dρ_i·Z_ij/N_i` cannot be made reciprocal by symmetric Z. That would require d_iρ_i/N_i to match across all coupled pairs (up to area factors), which is false wherever d and ρ differ, for example at boundaries. Reciprocity could therefore only be imposed by a loss. Its benefit to identifiability is INFERENCE.

## Q14. Does BGC avoid the failure mechanism of RSK (WL72 B)?

**Verdict.** Not for the dominant part. It removes two confounds that were present in RSK's evaluation.

- RSK's failure mechanism was never attributed (FACT, H9), so "avoids" can only be argued, not shown.
- A2 is essentially RSK's position-free relations plus frames and thickness (INFERENCE).
- The failing contexts are the side face, corners, UNS and TBE-far. They are out-of-orbit relative to S16(-R), and nothing in BGC supplies information about them (Q11).
- **Removed confound 1: the fixed A/O boundary fit floor of 0.27 (via A3).** But WL75 removed the angular floor and far transfer did not change (FACT).
- **Removed confound 2: orbit-related pair mismatch at rest (via A5; H10, H13).** But TBE-far, which has a second edge, and UNS are out-of-orbit.
- WL77A rejected H1 CQR, which is structurally close to A2+A5, for "out-of-orbit interpolation" (FACT about the rejection; I did not read its argument).
- **Drift.** Late-training specialisation recurs: the @2000 checkpoint beats the final one in WL72, 73 and 75, and Q78 rose from 0.63 to 0.86. More per-pair capacity likely amplifies it (INFERENCE).
- **New coupling not present in RSK.** The normalisation passes far-receiver errors into near-receiver values (D3).

## Q15. Strongest counterexample (orbit-consistent bump; DERIVATION)

- **Construction.**
  - Take any BGC instance θ that fits S16-R.
  - Choose φ ≥ 0, a smooth bump on orbit-invariant source-context descriptors. It vanishes on all interior contexts and on the D4h orbits of TBE, TBC, SM and SR, and it is nonzero at, for example, UNS or "8 mm from an edge".
  - Choose ψ, a non-uniform weighting over receivers or mixtures (for example emphasising side-face receivers). It must be non-uniform, because a uniform bump cancels in the normalisation.
  - Set `w' = w·(1 + λ·φ(c_i)·ψ(i,j,r))`.
- **What the modified instance still satisfies.**
  - It is linear, positive and passive (the normalisation holds).
  - It is exactly D4h-equivariant.
  - It contains no identity key.
  - It is identical on every training label.
- **What changes.** Held-out boundary responses change by O(1) in relative contrast.
- **Consequence.** BGC's hard constraints place no bound on exactly the errors that dominated WL72, 73, 75 and 78 (H9, H10). Only the encoder's inductive bias stands between the model and those errors, which is the same situation as RSK and W+UV-Q.
- **What BGC leaves unused (FACT H6).** Boundary responses are mostly interior shapes plus about ±5 % truncation. BGC does not use this as a constraint.

## Defects

- **D1. No identification for out-of-orbit boundary contexts (A2/A3/A6).** The architecture contains no mechanism that identifies the contexts where every historical model failed. Severity: high. DERIVATION plus FACT.
- **D2. A3 targets a secondary limit.** Per pair it is nearly uncompressed (648 vs 1,024 degrees of freedom), and it discards the shared global-angular prior and denoiser. The historical bottleneck to transfer was spatial (H7, H8). INFERENCE.
- **D3. A4 normalisation has four problems** (DERIVATION):
  1. It is incompatible with exact reciprocity by construction.
  2. It couples far-receiver Z errors into near-receiver K, and K(i,j) depends on the receiver set (tessellation, k-NN ties). This inflates the transport-dirty set for selective recompute.
  3. ε entangles ρ with the scale of Z.
  4. The local sparse part and the long-range part (A6) must share one denominator and have disjoint supports. Otherwise the passivity proof fails, or the two parts claim the same transport twice inside K.
- **D4. The A4 budget d is unreliable if it comes from Monte Carlo quadrature.** The bound is only relative to d_model, which is off by up to 0.075 (H14). A deterministic d is required. FACT plus DERIVATION.
- **D5. A1 moments are insufficient in two places.** They fail for chord mixtures near edges (κ₃ matters at about 1 mm scale) and at port or native resolution (rank 6–8). They are adequate at coarse level (H5). They also do not touch G. This agrees with WL77A's "sub-mfp information loss" objection to H2. DERIVATION.
- **D6. A2's endpoint-local context is insufficient under intervening deformation.** "Pairwise geometric context" is undefined. The context radius should be channel-dependent (about 1.6–3.5 mm screening, so 5–15 mm of context). DERIVATION.
- **D7. Leakage routes in A2/A6.** k-NN index ties (documented), the boundary-distance fingerprint, the face-dependent frame convention, first-triangle frames on dents, latents fitted per geometry, and absolute far-cell ids. FACT plus DERIVATION.
- **D8. A5 is exact only for rest D4h.** Dented poses are not exact under the current frame convention (H11), so the equivariance does not cover deformation. Averaging adds no out-of-orbit information. Averaging at inference costs about 16× (DERIVATION).
- **D9. A2's "material-domain information" clause.** For a homogeneous isotropic medium, K depends only on the current geometry. Admitting rest or absolute coordinates under this clause re-opens identity shortcuts. INFERENCE: the WL78 DEF regression is consistent with a shortcut being removed.
- **D10. Reciprocity is omitted.** It is an exact physical constraint that would turn sparse source labels into dense ones. It also conflicts with D3(1). DERIVATION.
- **D11. A6 state size (DERIVATION, order of magnitude).**
  - About 3,840 ports × 70–100 partners within 12 mm gives about 3×10⁵ pairs.
  - Each pair needs R·81·3 ≈ 1,944 floats, about 6×10⁸ floats (about 2.4 GB fp32) in total.
  - That is about 40× the ATO runtime state of 14.7M floats (WL72).
  - The alternative of caching latents and decoding on demand is unmeasured.

### Apply, per light (DERIVATION)

**Arithmetic.** With s(i,a,c) = x·d·ρ / denominator:

y(j,o,c) = Σ_i Σ_r w · v(i,j,r,o,c) · [Σ_a u(i,j,r,a,c) · s(i,a,c)]

- Per pair, this costs R·(A+O+1)·C multiply–adds: one per stored factor.
- Because v depends on the pair, it cannot be pulled out of the sum over sources.
- A global outgoing basis costs P·R·O·C once per receiver, plus R·C per pair. BGC therefore does **about 81× more arithmetic per pair** than a global ATO at equal N and R.

| Configuration | 1 light | 64 lights |
|---|---|---|
| N128 R8 | 9.6e8 MAC | 6.1e10 MAC (0.12 TFLOP) |
| N512 R16 | 7.6e9 MAC | 4.9e11 MAC (~1 TFLOP) |

**Lower bounds (INFERENCE; uses public specifications of about 912 GB/s and about 34 TFLOPS fp32, not measured).** TF32 stays off, following WL75/76 practice.
- 1 light is memory-bound at 0.5 FLOP per byte. N128 R8 needs at least about 4.2 ms, against A75's measured 1.37 ms on the 5080.
- 64 lights at N128 R8 need at least about 5–10 ms, but only with a fully fused, receiver-major gather kernel.

|---|---|---|

### Geometry rebuild (INFERENCE)

- **Full re-encode and decode:** about 10–30 ms (N128 R8) to 0.1–0.3 s (N512 R16). This is above A75's 8–15 ms and well below FIA's 75 s.
- **A1 statistics.** Chord moments in a homogeneous medium (collision probability 1−e^{−σℓ}, conditional mean and variance) are analytic per chord. With a deterministic port × bin quadrature of about 256 chords per cell, that is about 1.6e7 ray casts, i.e. milliseconds. If A1 is instead computed by Monte Carlo quadrature like WL76, the measured precedents are 75 s per rebuild and noise-driven spurious contrast (WL77A). This is unspecified in the proposal.
- **A5 group averaging.** If done by evaluating the generator on all 16 group images, encode and decode cost ×16: about 0.2–0.5 s (N128 R8) and about 1.6–5 s (N512 R16).
  - The averaged block is a sum of 16 rank-R terms with permuted frames, so it is generically full rank (16).
  - The R-factor cache then no longer holds. Storing 16R factors at N128 R8 would be 61 GB; storing the dense block is cheaper (6.04 GB).
  - Averaging is undefined on dented poses (WL79).
- **Bounds on selective reuse.**
  - Normalisation sums over all j′, so a local edit invalidates every source whose support contains a changed port (a dilation by the support radius, 13–24 mm).
  - Rebuilding geometry and B already dominates selective updates (WL59: 0.32 of 0.33 s; 34 % of edges updated at N128).

### Long-range energetics (DERIVATION; diffusion lowest slab mode, interior source, order of magnitude)

- D = 1/3 mm, extrapolation length 0.67 mm per side, H_eff = 11.3 mm. The decay length 1/κ is 3.5 / 3.1 / 1.6 mm for R / G / B.
- **128-NN** covers a radius of about 13.3 mm on the top face and about 8.2 mm on the bottom face.
  - The fraction of flux beyond that, from κR·K1(κR), is about **6 % for red reflection and about 22 % for red transmission**. Blue is about 0.1 % / 2 %.
  - At this N the long-range part is a first-order term, not a correction.
- **512-NN** (about 24 / 21 mm): red leaves less than 1 % beyond the support, but the cache is 15–31 GB.
- Edge and corner sources are not covered by this estimate.

## A6 — Sparse local pairs plus a long-range part with cached pair latents

**Verdict:**
- A6 is underspecified and partly self-defeating in the FNTO setting.
- Decoding only for "active illumination" saves little.
- The physics supports a separable far field: source factor u_far(i,a), a spatial kernel, and receiver factor v_far(j,o) (INFERENCE: past about 8 mean free paths angular memory is lost).

**Why active-illumination decoding does not help (DERIVATION and INFERENCE):**
- The A4 denominator needs u for every receiver j′ of each active source, so receivers cannot be pruned.
- C coupling needs K's output at every exiting port.
- A sun lights about 42 % of ports (1600 of 3840 top-face ports). Its single direction prunes only u, which is at most 20 % of the factors.
- From the second bounce on, C-produced incident fields fill all bins.

**Why the far field cannot be a simple global low-rank term (INFERENCE):** a global low-rank U·V of a kernel that decays over about 3.5 mm across a 100 mm domain needs a rank of roughly (100 / 3.5)² ≈ 800. The far part therefore has to be a hierarchical or coarse-cell term, such as WL76's 240-cell tail.

## Q13. Is BGC meaningfully different from existing conditional-transport and neural-operator methods?

**Verdict: NOT SUPPORTED as a new mechanism.** Every design element A1–A6 shares an exact mechanism with prior art, the project's own history, or both. Two things remain that might be new. One is the evaluated property: sparse-source transfer to held-out boundary ports and to deformed geometry, with port×bin correspondence. The other is one specific combination, listed under (d). Neither is a mechanism claim. (INFERENCE, resting on the derivations below and a search whose "nothing found" results are only weak negatives.)

### (a) The operator class adds no constraint (DERIVATION)

- **w and u collapse into one tensor.** Both w(i,j,r,c) and u(i,j,r,a,c) depend on the pair, so only their product f(i,j,r,a,c) = w·u can be identified. The split into a "spatial weight" and an "incoming angular response" is a gauge choice (w→λw, u→u/λ), not a constraint.
- **At R ≥ 16 the angular factorisation constrains nothing.** For each (i,j,c), the block Z(a,o) is a nonnegative 16×64 matrix of nonnegative rank at most R. Any nonnegative 16×64 matrix has nonnegative rank at most 16: take u = δ_{ra} times the row mass and v = the normalised row.
- **The normalisation constrains nothing beyond passivity.** Per (i,a,c), K = dρ·Z/(ε+ΣZ). Any nonnegative K whose column sums are at most d is representable, with ρ = column sum / d.
- **Consequence.** At R ≥ 16, A3+A4 spans exactly the full positive, passive cone. All of BGC's inductive bias then sits in the generator network (A1, A2) and in A5.
- **At R = 8 the constraint is probably weak (INFERENCE).** WL77A records that pair-specific angular blocks have rank of about 4. That is an SVD rank (a lower bound on nonnegative rank), and I did not re-measure it.

### (b) Exact shared mechanisms, element by element

**A3 + A4 operator** (per-source-column albedo × normalised conditional exit distribution, written as a mixture of separable lobes)
- 8DNA (arXiv:2604.25129; V-WL77A) uses F = α(x_o,ω_o)·p(x_i,ω_i | x_o,ω_o): the same mass × normalised-distribution pattern, written in the adjoint direction, with a flow instead of a mixture. It is per-asset.
- Vicini–Koltun–Jakob 2019, "A learned shape-adaptive subsurface scattering model" (TOG 38(4); V-this-session via the SIGGRAPH archive): a shared absorption model plus a conditional exit sampler, conditioned on local geometry, that generalises to unseen shapes.
- Neural Parametric Mixtures (Dong, Wang, Li, SIGGRAPH 2023; V-this-session): a position-conditioned network decodes normalised parametric mixtures.
- Mixture density networks (Bishop 1994; U).
- "Conditional low rank" is the DeepONet branch×trunk form with a conditioned factor, i.e. a hypernetwork-generated low-rank head (DeepONet: V-WL77A; low-rank HyperDeepONet arXiv 2507.18346: snippet level only, this session).
- **Within the project:** global ATO K = d Σ_r A[r,a] T[i,j,r] O[r,o] (WL68 code-derived family; WL74/75) is BGC with u and v made pair-independent. BGC is the hypernetwork generalisation of ATO (FACT for ATO's form; DERIVATION for the relation).

**A1 incident encoder**
- The first-collision factorisation is classical: the first-collision source method from neutron transport, Jensen 2001, and Photon Beam Diffusion 2013 (V-WL77A). The project has already implemented it as FIA (WL76 Stage 0: K_scat = (1−β)·E_{x∼p1}[Q(x→j,o)], validated 36/36).
- DeepRTE (arXiv:2505.23190; V-this-session, from the HTML): an attention "OpticalDepthNet" encodes first-flight attenuation along characteristics. A learned Green's function G(r,Ω,r′,Ω′) is integrated against the inflow, i.e. linear superposition, and is pre-trained on delta-like inflows.
- DeepRTE has no positivity or energy constraint. Its Remark 3.1 says a new geometry needs retraining, and its experiments use a square domain. So BGC differs from it in geometry conditioning and passivity, not in the Green's-function-from-delta-inflow mechanism.
- Collision probability is already d = 1 − β, so that A1 feature is redundant (FACT: WL76 E[c] = 1 − β).

**A2 relation encoder**
- **Within the project:** this is essentially W+UV-Q (WL78, FACT). WL78 removed absolute position, face id and material-axis offsets, and added inputs in the bin frame: rest→current displacement, area, thickness and normal spread. It added two boundary probes with saturating weights max(0, 1 − r/20 mm)² and max(0, 1 − r/5 mm)², and pair offsets in the source bin frame. Result: INCONCLUSIVE. BND_FAR con_coarse was 0.668 / 0.860 against A75's 0.902 / 0.945, and deformation was worse than A75 (abs_coarse 0.31–0.65 vs 0.26–0.48).
- **Prior art:** Vicini 2019 (local polynomial around the incident point), Deep Scattering 2017 (descriptor relative to the shading point and the light) and EGNN-style relative features (V-WL77A).

**A5 frame transformation**
- Frame averaging (Puny et al., ICLR 2022, arXiv:2110.03336; V-this-session). For the finite rest group D4h (16 elements), frame averaging over the whole group is the Reynolds group average.
- **Within the project:** the exact maps K(gG) = P_out K(G) P_in⁻¹ were built and validated in WL79 Stage 0 (FACT).
- Equivariance by construction: G-FNO, and zonal-harmonic rotation in LDPRT (V-WL77A).

**A6 representation**
- Local sparse interactions plus a separate long-range part is the project's existing W (128-NN) + UV low-rank tail split. Prior art for that split: H-matrices, Multipole GNO, hierarchical radiosity (V-WL77A).
- Cached pair latents correspond to cached transfer coefficients, as in PRT/CPCA and the WL59 F/S coefficient cache.

**Neural radiance caching (Müller 2021) and TransGI**
- NRC is an online, nonlinear radiance cache, not an operator. BGC does not share its core mechanism; the overlap is only a generic idea of caching latents (DERIVATION).
- TransGI (arXiv 2506.09909) shares the object-centric transfer plus runtime-coupling class, i.e. the K/C split, and nothing in A1–A5. It has no released code (FACT, per the baseline-roles document).

**Element Learning** (Du & Stechmann, arXiv 2308.02467; V-this-session)
- A learned, geometry-conditioned boundary-to-boundary "in2out" operator for the RTE that applies to new geometries without retraining. Its elements are small and composed by an HDG solver.
- It is the closest structural precedent for "geometry-conditioned learned boundary operator" (INFERENCE).

### (c) The one element new relative to the project's history targets a non-binding factor

The only part of BGC that is new relative to WL57–80 is A3's pair-conditioned angular factors. The record ranks angular capacity as secondary:

- **Bound (DERIVATION).** Even a perfect angular family can remove at most about 0.159 of the 0.80 BND4_FAR error. The remaining ~0.64 lies in the learned spatial/coefficient map.

## Q15. Strongest counterexample (prior-art and history angle)

**Verdict: BGC's stated constraints (A4 passivity, A5 symmetry, positivity) leave its out-of-orbit boundary transfer unidentified by the training data.** That transfer therefore depends entirely on the generator's inductive bias, which is the same situation that produced the WL68 / 71 / 72 / 75 / 78 negatives. (DERIVATION plus INFERENCE.)

### Hand-constructed counterexample (DERIVATION)

**Setup.**
- Let s* be a held-out boundary source near an edge. Its context, for example the distance to the perpendicular face, matches no training source's orbit. (FACT: the training boundary sources sit 1.8–4.0 mm from the perpendicular face, WL79.)
- Assume no training port, and no orbit image of one, lies within 5 mm of s*. This is my assumption about "far", not checked against every population.

**Two operators that agree on all the data.**
- K1 is the true operator.
- K2 equals K1 except on the columns of orbit(s*). There, for receivers within 5 mm only, each receiver's outgoing distribution is replaced by its reflection about the in-face axis perpendicular to the edge, and the reflection is conjugated consistently across the orbit.

**Why the data and constraints cannot separate them.**
- Both are positive. Both have identical ρ and identical per-receiver mass, so both are passive.
- Both are exactly D4h-equivariant.
- Both reproduce every supervised column and its images.
- Helmholtz reciprocity does not distinguish them either. The modified entries pair with source columns inside the 5 mm ball, and none of those is supervised.
- They differ exactly in near-field contrast, the "con" metrics where every historical model fails. The ~5 mm near field carries 99.0–99.9 % of the native signal (WL78 D1, FACT).
- Only RTE physics distinguishes K1 from K2. BGC's learned parts receive that physics only through features: A1 summarises it with moments, and A2 only approximately.

**Two side derivations.**
- **A1 moments are not sufficient statistics where it matters.** E[Q] needs the whole first-collision measure. Mean and covariance capture it only up to a second-order Taylor expansion of Q. Near the source, Q varies on the ~1 mm mean-free-path scale, which is also the spread of the first-collision density, so the truncation is O(1) in the near field.
- **The translate counterexample is already covered.** On the physically identical translates (WL78 D1), W+UV-Q removed the absolute-coordinate pathway (origin-offset change ≤ 1e-4). Yet its near-field mechanism criterion still failed: equivalent-context difference Q78/A75 = 1.00 / 1.03 (FACT, WL78). A2's main promise has therefore been tested once and did not deliver.

### Reciprocity as a partial (non-)remedy

- Reciprocity is an exact physical constraint that BGC does not list. In principle it turns the 16 supervised source columns into 16 known rows (dual-photography transposition: Sen et al., SIGGRAPH 2005, V-this-session) (DERIVATION).
- Two limits apply. The 16-in / 64-out bins first have to be aggregated to matched cells. And per the construction above, reciprocity constrains only entries at supervised ports, i.e. the far field for boundary-far sources (INFERENCE).

## Defects found

1. **A3 (identifiability).** w and u are not separately identifiable, because the product of two pair-conditioned factors is one free tensor. At R ≥ 16 the factorisation is unconstrained (nonnegative rank ≤ 16). "Conditional angular-spatial operator" is therefore an output head, not a structural prior, and claims that interpret w as spatial and u as angular are void. (DERIVATION)

2. **A3 (target mismatch).** A3 adds angular freedom where the record puts the binding error elsewhere. The BND4_FAR oracle with a fixed global basis reaches 0.159, against 0.80–0.82 learned (WL75). The orientation gap is ≤ 1.41× (WL79) and ≤ 1.15× after orbit balancing (WL80). Conditioning the angular factors on the pair also exposes them to the geometry→coefficient generalisation failure (WL68 corr ≈ 0). (FACT plus INFERENCE)

3. **A4 × A6 (energy partition).** The denominator Σ_{j′,o′} Z runs over every receiver, but A6 handles long range with a separate representation.
   - If local and long-range parts are each normalised to dρ, the sum can reach 2dρ and passivity fails.
   - If only the local part is normalised, the missing energy is pushed into the near field. WL78 D3 measures the collided energy outside the 128-NN support (radius 12.5–17 mm) at 5.5 / 3.2 / 0.1 % for interior sources (RGB), so this would bias red interior near-field mass by about +5.5 %.
   - The proposal does not say how ρ is split between the two parts. (DERIVATION from FACT)

4. **A4 (meaning of ρ).** Through ε, the column sum is dρ·ΣZ/(ε+ΣZ) < dρ. ρ is then not the realised outgoing fraction, which compounds with defect 3. ρ is also free in [0, 1], although physically the total exit fraction per (i,a,c) is a determined quantity tied to albedo and geometry; it is a learned scalar with no physical anchor. (DERIVATION)

5. **A1 (sufficiency and redundancy).** Collision probability duplicates d = 1 − β (WL76). Mean and covariance of the first-collision density are not sufficient for E[Q], and fail precisely in the ~mean-free-path near field. The exact factorisation needs the full measure, as FIA did by quadrature. (DERIVATION)

6. **A2 (absolute information re-entering).** In a 100×100×10 mm box, the vector of signed distances to the six faces is an affine bijection of absolute position. Unclipped "boundary distances" would reopen the WL78 identity pathway, in which V looks up a global receiver pattern keyed by the source. The proposal does not state the clipping.
   - WL78 used saturating 20 mm and 5 mm probe weights (FACT).
   - A rough diffusion estimate puts the lateral decay length at about 3.6 mm for albedo 0.999 in a 10 mm slab (DERIVATION, approximate). That figure supplies a physical justification for a cut-off around 20 mm, which the proposal should state.
   - A2 "must not discard physically meaningful absolute information" and "must not key on source identity" conflict in a finite box unless a range is specified.

7. **A5 (scope).** Group averaging adds information only for held-out sources inside the orbit of a training source. For an out-of-orbit s*, it averages 16 out-of-sample evaluations (see the Q15 construction). It is undefined, or at best approximate, for deformed poses: WL79 shows the current first-triangle bin frames give non-bijective outgoing maps for the 90° rotations and the diagonal reflection (bin residual 0.06–0.16). Whole-geometry rotation is already exact through the material frame (WL78 class 1). A5 therefore covers only the rest-geometry D4h orbit, which WL79/80 already handle by exact labels, and it costs 16× in network evaluations. (FACT plus DERIVATION)

8. **Physical validity (omission).** Reciprocity is not among BGC's constraints and is not preserved by per-column normalisation. It is the only listed-or-unlisted exact constraint that couples supervised columns to unsupervised ones, though mainly in the far field. (DERIVATION; prior art: dual photography)

9. **Novelty claim (overall).** Every element maps to prior art or project history: 8DNA / Vicini / NPM; DeepONet / hypernetworks; FIA / first-collision source method / DeepRTE; W+UV-Q; frame averaging; W+UV / H-matrix. Any contribution would be empirical only, per RESEARCH_CENTRIC_TOPIC.md l.41 and FNTO contract §8. (INFERENCE)

**3. MAJOR: the A4 normalisation creates harmful non-identifiability and breaks reciprocity.** (DERIVATION)
- **ρ is redundant.** Physically, the total exit per (i,a,c) equals Σ_{j,o} K. A separately predicted ρ duplicates it, and through ε it trades off against |Z|, so ρ is not anchored to any physical quantity.
- **A uniform per-(i,a) rescaling of Z cancels**, so any regulariser on Z has no fixed scale.
- **Exact reciprocity holds only if ρ_i ∝ S_i/(m_i d_i).** The oracle K is reciprocal to 0.998–1.000 (AGENTS.md WL53 summary), but per-column normalisation with an independent ρ is generically non-reciprocal.
- **This forfeits the one exact constraint that could densify labels.** Through reciprocity, the 16 supervised columns would also give 16 rows (bin nesting is INFERENCE).
- **Edits far from a pair still change it.** An edit outside a pair's context rescales every near receiver by the same factor, (ρ′/ρ)(S/S′). The physical change there is about exp(−excess path/λ), which is near 0. The physics audit's hypothetical example gives about −5 %. This damages the K side of transport-dirty localisation.

**4. MAJOR: state and apply cost do not fit a resident runtime at the capacity the evidence points to.** (DERIVATION; timings INFERENCE)
- **Storage reaches the dense block at R ≥ 13.** Each pair needs 243·R floats, against 3,072 floats for a dense block.
- **Cache sizes are large.**
  - N128/R8 needs 3.82 GB in fp32. That is about 130× the WL76 A75 state (29.5 M floats) and about 32× the WL75 rank-16 ATO.
  - N512/R16 needs 30.6 GB and does not fit a 12 GB or 16 GB GPU.
- **The 128-neighbour support leaves a first-order far term.** Diffusion estimates put 6–22 % of red flux beyond it. WL78 D3 measured 5.5 % outside the 128-NN support for interior sources, so the long-range representation must be accurate.
- **Apply is about 81× a global ATO per pair** (A+O+1 multiply–adds), because a pair-dependent v cannot be factored out of the source sum. A naive einsum needs about 24 GB of intermediates. Timing precedents:
  - WL76 dense scatter: 3.07 s;
  - WL76 with pre-assembled CSR operators: 0.25–0.35 s;
  - WL75 rank-16 ATO, one light: 1.37 ms.
- **The latent-only alternative is slow.** Decoding costs about 15–30 ms per K application at N128/R8, and about 0.1–0.2 s at N512/R16. With 6–20 applications per coupled solve, that is about 0.1–4 s per frame (INFERENCE).
- **Decoding only for active illumination saves little.** The denominator needs every receiver j′, C needs every exiting port, and a sun prunes only u, which is at most about 20 % of the factors.

**5. MAJOR: A5 averaging is exact only at rest, multiplies cost by 16, and breaks the factor cache.** (FACT + DERIVATION)
- On dented poses, the maps have bin residual 0.06–0.16 and non-bijective outgoing maps (FACT, WL79), so the averaging is undefined there.
- The averaged pair block is a sum of 16 permuted rank-R terms, so it is generically full rank. A dense cache then becomes cheaper than the factor cache.
- Averaging adds no information outside the orbit (objection 1).

**6. MAJOR: the A1 features are insufficient and their source is unspecified.** (DERIVATION + FACT)
- Moments (d, μ, Σ) cannot close single scatter. The depth-factor error of a second-order closure is +10 % at μ_i = μ_o and +22 % at 2×. Single scatter carries 25–36 % of the collided energy (WL76 post hoc diagnostic).
- A1 is source-only, but the exact bilinear form needs the receiver-side first-collision density as well.
- "Collision probability" duplicates d = 1 − β.
- If the features come from Monte Carlo quadrature, they inherit the measured costs (FACT): a 75 s rebuild (WL76), q = 1024 single-scatter noise at 0.45–0.48 of the multiple-scatter norm (WL77A), and β error up to 0.075 absolute (WL74A).

**7. MAJOR: the selective-recompute contract degrades.** (INFERENCE + FACT)
- The pairwise context and the global denominator widen invalidation to the edit region dilated by the support radius (about 13–24 mm).
- Geometry and B rebuild already account for about 0.32 of the 0.33 s selective update (WL59).
- No cost advantage can be claimed. Any future cost comparison must also go through the mandatory baseline gate in `docs/BASELINE_ROLES_AND_EVIDENCE_STRATEGY.md`; Neural Full Recompute ≠ Cycles.

**8. MINOR: physical bounds and measures are underspecified.** (DERIVATION/INFERENCE)
- The bound should be ω(c)·d, not d.
- Port-area and bin-measure weights are missing from ΣZ, which is wrong under deformation.
- The optical domain of the normalisation is unrestricted, so positive activations give leaks.
- The local and far parts of A6 have no shared, disjoint budget (a τ-type split is needed).
- The density law for σ_t under deformation is unspecified.

**9. MINOR: no mechanism is novel.** (INFERENCE)
- BGC is the hypernetwork generalisation of ATO (u, v made pair-dependent).
- A1 is FIA, A2 is about W+UV-Q / RSK, A5 is frame averaging, and A6 is W + UV.
- The only part new to the project's history, pair-conditioned angular factors, targets a factor that WL75/80 rank as non-binding. A perfect angular family could remove at most about 0.159 of the roughly 0.80 BND4_FAR error.

## Strongest counterexample

**Setup.** Take any BGC instance θ that fits all S16-R labels.
- Let φ ≥ 0 be a smooth bump on orbit-invariant source-context descriptors. It vanishes on every interior context and on the D4h orbits of TBE, TBC, SM and SR. It is nonzero on out-of-orbit boundary contexts, for example UNS (the vertical-edge group) or 8 mm from an edge.
- Let ψ be a non-uniform receiver or mixture weighting, for example one that emphasises side-face receivers. It must be non-uniform, because a uniform bump cancels in the denominator.
- Set w′ = w·(1 + λφψ).

**What θ′ still satisfies.**
- It is linear, nonnegative and passive (the normalisation holds).
- It is exactly D4h-equivariant.
- It has no identity key.
- Its training loss equals θ's.
- Reciprocity does not separate it from θ either: the modified entries pair only with unsupervised columns.

**What changes.** Held-out boundary near-field contrast changes by O(1). That near field is where 99.0–99.9 % of the native signal lies (WL78 D1, FACT), and it is the metric on which WL72, WL75 and WL78 failed.

Nothing in BGC prefers θ over θ′; only optimiser bias decides. The record shows that bias landing on the wrong side: 0.80 learned against 0.159 oracle (WL75), and 0.668/0.860 seed-dependent with late drift (WL78).

**Runtime half.** Suppose the right instance were found anyway. The only rank evidence is global: rank 16 was needed for the WL59 gates as an oracle (WL74). Per-pair nonnegative rank has never been measured. At R ≥ 13 the factor cache is larger than the dense local block. At the support size that keeps the far term below 1 % (N ≈ 512), the cache is 15–31 GB, beyond both test GPUs.

## Recommendation: REJECT

Reject as a training or implementation candidate in its current form. Reconsideration would need each of the following answered beforehand by derivation or a non-learned check on existing references. This lists what a revision would have to answer and authorises nothing.

1. **Identification.** A stated mechanism, not an encoder hope, that constrains out-of-orbit boundary contexts. Candidates include:
   - reciprocity-consistent normalisation that turns supervised columns into rows;
   - computing the exactly computable first-collision part rather than learning it;
   - a broader supervised context population.

   The proposal must show it defeats the bump construction.
2. **Memorisation controls.** Encoder-only latents, clipped boundary descriptors, tie-free neighbour sets, a single frame convention, and a predeclared checkpoint and drift rule.
3. **A4 fix.** ρ removed or tied to ΣZ, a bound of ω·d, area and bin measures, domain restriction, and a single local/far budget.
4. **Per-pair nonnegative rank at target accuracy**, split into near, edge/corner and far pairs. This decides whether the cost objection stands: if R ≤ 4 suffices, N128 needs 1.9 GB; if R ≥ 13, BGC is a neural generator of a dense block.
5. **Cost budget.** A fused-kernel apply and rebuild budget against A75/ATO on 12 GB, with A5 cost and its behaviour off the rest geometry stated.

**Status of the evidence.** No measurement in this review is new. The cost figures are derivations or inferences from public specifications and the WL59/75/76 precedents, and the per-pair rank is UNMEASURED.
