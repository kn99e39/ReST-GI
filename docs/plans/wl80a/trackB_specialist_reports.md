## 1. What the measured outcomes do and do not establish

**Established (FACT, taken from the brief):**
- **Fitting is not the bottleneck.** Every learned operator reaches the gates, or comes close, on the sources it was supervised on. None of them reaches the gates on independent boundary sources.
- **The physical response is local.** For boundary sources, 99.0–99.9 % of the response norm lies within 5 mm. A context is set by the distance to the nearest boundary. Moving the second-nearest boundary from 18.75 mm to 41–49 mm leaves the response unchanged within noise.
- **The learned operators break exact equivalences that the references obey.** Symmetry-equivalent contexts differ by 12–33 % (native) and 42–141 % (fine16 contrast). Adding a constant shift to the absolute-coordinate inputs changes boundary predictions by 10–23 %.
- **A fixed, globally shared angular-profile basis fits boundary sources less well, even in a per-source oracle.** On exact symmetry images it is 1.26–1.41× worse. Refitting over the orbit narrows the gap to ≤ 1.15× but costs 2–18 % in the original orientation.
- **Anchoring at the first interaction works.** The factorisation itself is exact. The apparent angular floor (0.38–0.45) was confounded by q = 1,024 quadrature noise; noise-free estimates are about 0.07.
- **Transfer to unsupervised sources peaks early** (500–2,000 of 20,000 steps) and then degrades, while the supervised fit keeps improving.

**Not established:**
- Why boundary transfer fails. Data volume, architecture, invariance and optimisation have not been separated.
- Whether any learned source-conditioned map could transfer from 16 sources.
- Whether the angular limit is fundamental.
- Whether the deformation-correlation loss of the geometry-relative variant is systematic. Two seeds is not enough to say.

**INFERENCE (consistent with the data, not shown by it):** every approach tried has learned a map whose input includes the source position or context, and whose output must reproduce how the incoming beam direction interacts with nearby faces. With 16 sources, the boundary contexts (side rims, vertical edges, corners) are covered by only a handful of examples. Early-best transfer followed by degradation is the usual signature of memorising per-source behaviour.

**DERIVATION (the key physical fact that I believe the tried approaches did not use):** for a homogeneous, index-matched medium with an isotropic phase function, a photon keeps no memory of its incoming direction after its first collision. All of the dependence of K on the incoming bin a, and therefore all of `con_*`, enters only through the first-collision density q1(x; i, a). All of the outgoing-bin dependence enters only through the last flight from the final collision site to the exit. Between those two points, transport is a scalar, direction-free kernel between two interior points. That kernel is source-independent and is fixed exactly by the Peierls integral equation. (FACT: the Peierls collision-density equation is exact for isotropic scattering. Case and Zweifel; Bell and Glasstone.)

## 2. Mechanisms

### Mechanism A (preferred): a "flight sandwich" around an exact scalar collision resolvent (collision-probability K)

**Operator.** Let m and n index collision sites, which are voxels of the medium Ω. Let c index the channel and ω_c the single-scattering albedo.

```
K[i,a,j,o,c] = ω_c · Σ_{m,n} A_out[(j,o), n] · R_c[n, m] · F_in[m, (i,a)]
R_c = (I − ω_c W)^{-1}
```

- **F_in[m,(i,a)]** is the probability that light entering port i in bin a has its first collision in voxel m. It is the integral over the port area and the bin's solid angle of σ_t·e^{-σ_t s}, ray-marched inside Ω. It is explicit and depends on geometry only. It sums to exactly d(i, a) if it uses the same rays as the ballistic operator B.
- **W[n,m]** is the probability that a photon scattered isotropically in m next collides in n, with the chord required to stay inside Ω. It is explicit, depends on geometry only and is the same for every channel, because σ_t is equal in all channels (FACT).
- **A_out[(j,o),n]** is the probability that a photon scattered in n leaves without further collision through port j in bin o. It is explicit and built from the same rays as W, so for every n: e(n) = Σ_{j,o} A_out = 1 − Σ_m W[m,n].
- **Nothing is learned.** The channel enters only through the scalar ω_c.

**Source and geometry conditioning.** No learned function ever sees the source. A new source only changes F_in, which is computed exactly. Geometry enters through:
- the medium mask (the voxel set of Ω);
- per-port ray setup for F_in and A_out;
- internal visibility in W.

**Representation.**
- *Space:* voxels, with h ≈ 0.5 mm (half a mean free path), optionally h_z ≈ 0.25 mm.
- *Angle:* none inside the medium (DERIVATION: isotropic scattering). Angle exists only in the analytic boundary flights, integrated exactly over each bin with stratified quadrature that is deterministic and noise-free.

**Guaranteed invariants (DERIVATION).**
- *Linearity, zero-in/zero-out, non-negativity.* All factors are non-negative. 0 ≤ ω_c W is sub-stochastic, so the Neumann series converges to a non-negative R_c.
- *Passivity, exactly in discrete form.* Let f = R_c F_in, the collision density. Then Σ f = d + ω Σ(1 − e) f, so the total output is ω Σ e f = d(i, a) − (1 − ω) Σ f ≤ d(i, a). This holds because W and A_out share one ray set.
- *Exact rest symmetries.* Place the voxel grid symmetrically (100×100×10 mm at 0.5 mm voxels is symmetric under D4h) and orbit-symmetrise the quadrature sample sets. Then K(g·i, g·a, g·j, g·o) = K(i, a, j, o) exactly for all 16 operations g.
- *Port/bin correspondence.* Bins are used only as integration domains of the analytic flights, so a per-port frame convention cannot introduce a mismatch.
- *Dented poses* need no symmetry assumption.
- *Reciprocity.* W is symmetric for equal voxel volumes. If F_in and A_out are computed from mirrored ray sets, discrete reciprocity holds up to the stored cosine, area and solid-angle factors. This is optional.
- *Ownership.* Only paths inside Ω are counted. The first exit terminates the path, and air re-entry belongs to C.
- *Ballistic part.* B and the collided budget d are untouched.

**Transformation under geometry change.**
- *Rigid motion:* K is invariant in the body frame.
- *Dent:* update the mask, then recompute F_in and A_out for the affected ports, in milliseconds. The FFT of the free-space kernel does not change, because the medium is homogeneous.
- *Internal visibility (DERIVATION):* where a dent makes the medium locally non-convex, a chord between two sub-surface points leaves Ω only if the sagitta r²/(8R_c) exceeds the points' depth. For depth 6 mm and radius 15–25 mm, the curvature radius R_c is about 22–36 mm, so the sagitta is ≈ 0.03–0.05 mm at r = 3 mm. The masked-convolution error is therefore confined to a sliver within ~0.1 mm of the dent surface. It can be corrected exactly by ray-marching a thin band if needed. The brief does not give the dent profile, so a sharp rim could be worse (HYPOTHESIS).
- *Signed deformation responses* follow from the physics, not from a fit. This should remove the deformation-correlation failure mode (HYPOTHESIS until tested).

**Why it transfers to unseen sources (mechanism, not capacity).**
- The only source-dependent factor is the exact first flight.
- The resolvent is a property of the body, solved everywhere, corners included, with no source supervision.
- The measured context equivalence (FACT 3a) is reproduced automatically, because R_c is the true Green's function of the body.
- The boundary-near contrast arises where a perpendicular face truncates a tilted first-collision cloud. Both factors here are exact: F_in and the boundary in W and A_out.
- What remains is discretisation error: flat source per voxel, near-field integration of the kernel, bin quadrature. It shrinks under refinement and does not depend on whether a context was supervised.

**Training supervision.** None. The Monte Carlo references serve only for validation and for grid-convergence checks. The 4 GPU-minutes per source go into evaluation, not fitting.

### Mechanism B (learned and compressed): a geometry-mode or context-library resolvent with the same exact flights

Mechanism B keeps F_in and A_out exactly as in A and replaces R_c with a compressed form that is cheap at runtime.

**Collision sites.** Use a boundary-layer site basis m: per port column, L_d = 4–6 depth functions (exponential/Laguerre decay in depth). That gives M ≈ 15k–23k sites.

**Building the resolvent R_c[n, m].** Rather than learning it from source responses, build it from solves of Mechanism A, one per *context class*:
- interior columns: one translation class;
- side-face columns, by distance to the face (0–8 ports, 2.5–20 mm, roughly R's diffusion length);
- edge and corner columns, by distance pairs, further reduced by D4h.

That is a few hundred solves. Columns for every other site follow by exact translation or symmetry. This is the measured context equivalence (FACT 3a) turned into an explicit indexing rule.

**Learning (optional).** Learning is used only to:
- (i) interpolate R_c over continuous dent parameters (centre, depth, radius), trained on the Peierls residual or on solves from Mechanism A, never on per-source Monte Carlo;
- (ii) compress R_c. Two options:
  - sparse truncation within 20 mm: ~1,600 neighbours × 15k sites × 3 channels ≈ 7e7 entries, about 0.3 GB, with a sparse matrix-vector product well under 1 ms;
  - an albedo-generic spectral form R_c = U diag(1/(1 − ω_c λ_k)) Uᵀ on the channel-free eigenmodes of W. My estimate is about 10²–10³ slow modes with λ > 0.9 in this box, plus explicit low collision orders (SPECULATION about the exact count).

**Invariants.** Non-negativity and passivity survive compression only if the compressed R_c stays element-wise non-negative and keeps the column sums of the solve. Enforce this by row-sum renormalisation against e(n), which is computed exactly. Spectral truncation can produce negative entries and needs clamping, which breaks exactness.

**Why it transfers.** Coverage comes from context classes and exact symmetries of the geometry, not from source samples. A new source is again only a new F_in.

## 3. Arguments against my own preferences

**Against Mechanism A:**
1. **It is not a learned representation; it is a deterministic transport solver.** Its value depends on cost. The table puts one application at ~10–150 ms, against a ms-scale learned operator, and path-traced re-rendering of a small workload at matched quality takes a few seconds. The coupled warm-started solve may close the gap (HYPOTHESIS). But voxel count scales as volume / h³. An optically thick object of metre scale at σ_t = 1000 m⁻¹ is intractable at h ≈ mean free path / 2 without a multiscale scheme (diffusion in the bulk, transport in boundary layers). The proposal may only shift the problem.
2. **The physical simplicity is specific to this domain.** It relies on an isotropic phase function, an index-matched boundary and homogeneity.
   - Anisotropic scattering brings back angular memory: R must carry P_N moments, giving a matrix-valued Green's function.
   - Fresnel internal reflection couples A_out back into the volume.
   - Heterogeneity breaks the FFT convolution.
   The mechanism "transfers" because it encodes physics exactly. Outside that physics it offers no generalisation argument of its own.
3. **Discretisation error may itself be stratified by boundary context.** Flat-source collision-probability error is largest where the collision density is steepest: within ~1 mean free path of the entry point, and near faces, edges and corners. That is exactly BND_NEAR and BND_FAR. Converging there may need h ≤ 0.25 mm near the surface (1.6–3.2e6 voxels) or a non-uniform grid, which breaks the plain FFT. If so, the same boundary pattern could reappear as numerical error (HYPOTHESIS).
4. **High albedo makes convergence fragile.** R (ω = 0.999) needs acceleration. DSA for collision-probability formulations is well established in reactor physics, but it adds implementation risk.
5. **Contract mismatch is possible.** If the reference's "port/bin" packet is not uniform over port area and bin solid angle, an exact F_in will disagree with it for reasons unrelated to transport.

**Against Mechanism B:**
- A lateral site resolution of 2.5 mm is too coarse for the ≤ 5 mm near field that carries 99 % of the boundary response. Refining laterally ×4 pushes the sparse resolvent to ~1e9 entries.
- Compression can break exact passivity and symmetry unless it is renormalised.
- Learning over dent parameters reintroduces an interpolation-generalisation question, now in shape space rather than source space.

## 4. Closest prior work (as precisely and honestly as I can)

**Reactor physics: the strongest precedent.**
- *Collision-probability and Peierls integral methods* (Bell and Glasstone, *Nuclear Reactor Theory*, 1970; Case and Zweifel, *Linear Transport Theory*, 1967; lattice codes of the WIMS and DRAGON type) compute exactly the first-flight / collision-resolvent / escape structure of Mechanism A on spatial cells.
- *Response-matrix and interface-current methods* (Lindahl and Weiss, review in *Advances in Nuclear Science and Technology*, ~1981) define per-node operators from incoming partial currents to outgoing partial currents with space and angle expansions. This is conceptually K's contract (boundary in to first boundary out, per domain).
- *COMET.* The Incident Flux Response Expansion Method for heterogeneous coarse-mesh transport (2006; I attribute it to Mosher and Rahnema from memory, and the search confirmed the title and year but not the authors) precomputes local response functions, by Monte Carlo or deterministic transport, for orthogonal-polynomial expansions of the incident boundary flux in space and angle, then couples nodes iteratively. Forget and Rahnema (ANS Transactions, 2005) studied high-order spatial expansions of the incident current.
- *Diffusion-synthetic acceleration* for high-albedo iteration is standard (Alcouffe, 1977; Adams and Larsen, *Progress in Nuclear Energy*, 2002).

**Graphics, subsurface scattering:**
- Jensen et al. (2001), dipole BSSRDF.
- Donner and Jensen (2005), multipole for thin slabs (images across extrapolated boundaries).
- d'Eon and Irving (2011), quantized diffusion.
- Habel, Christensen and Jarosz (2013), photon beam diffusion. It integrates the diffusion response along the incident beam: an approximate version of the F_in sandwich.
- Frisvad, Hachisuka and Kjeldsen (2014), directional dipole: incident-direction dependence.
- Contini, Martelli and Zaccanti (1997), image-based diffusion in turbid slabs.
- Finite-element subsurface: Haber et al. (2005); Arbree, Walter and Bala (2011).
- Wang et al. (2005), all-frequency precomputed transport for translucent objects.
- Wang et al. (2008), polygrid diffusion.
- Moon, Walter and Marschner (2007), precomputed shell transport for discrete random media.
- Loos et al. (2011), modular radiance transfer: per-module precomputed transport with interfaces, close in spirit to the K/C split.

**Learned subsurface and volume transport:**
- Vicini, Koltun and Jakob (2019), a learned shape-adaptive subsurface scattering model. It conditions a sampler of exit positions on a local polynomial fit of the geometry around the entry point: local, source-relative conditioning.
- Leonard, Höhlein and Westermann (CGF 40(2), 2021), "Learning Multiple-Scattering Solutions for Sphere-Tracing of Volumetric Subsurface Effects" (verified). Conditional VAEs learn photon-path statistics from a point to a sphere boundary, used inside sphere tracing.
- Kallweit et al. (2017), Deep Scattering (radiance-predicting networks for clouds).
- Physics-informed RTE: Mishra and Molinaro (2021, JQSRT).

**What is plausibly new (INFERENCE).** Mechanism A's ingredients are classical. What seems new is the use: a reusable per-domain K for dynamic GI that has exact discrete passivity, bin correspondence and D4h equivariance at the port/bin contract; keeps the collided budget consistent with a separate ballistic operator; and is coupled into an explicit free-space solve with the collision densities as joint unknowns. Mechanism B's "context-class resolvent plus exact flights", where measured context equivalence becomes the indexing rule, is also not a standard graphics construction as far as I know.

## 2. Mechanisms

Notation: i is the source port, a the incoming bin (16), j the receiver port, o the outgoing bin (64), c the channel, and x, y are volume cells.

### Mechanism A (preferred): collision-space reciprocal factorisation (CSF)

**Exact identity (DERIVATION).** It follows from four conditions in the brief:
- the phase function is isotropic;
- the medium is homogeneous;
- σ_t is the same in every channel and only α_c differs;
- the boundary is index-matched, so a photon that reaches it leaves.

After any collision, the photon's future depends only on the collision position, not on its history. Therefore:

```
K_c[i,a,j,o] = Σ_x Σ_y  Φin[i,a,x] · α_c [(I − α_c W)^{-1}]_{x y} · Φout[y,j,o]
             = Σ_{n≥0} α_c^{n+1} T_n[i,a,j,o],   with T_n = Φin W^n Φout
```

The three factors are:
- **Φin[i,a,x]**: the probability that light entering uniformly over port i, in bin a, using the reference's emission convention, has its *first* collision in cell x. Σ_x Φin[i,a,x] = 1 − β(i,a) = d(i,a) exactly, so the collided budget and the separate ballistic operator B are consistent by construction.
- **W[x,y]**: the probability that isotropic emission uniformly in cell x has its next collision in cell y. Flights that leave Ω, including chords through the dent cavity, are excluded.
- **Φout[y,j,o]**: the probability that isotropic emission in y escapes uncollided through port j in bin o. Escape is tallied as power, which matches the reference tally.

Row conservation holds by construction: Σ_y W[x,y] + Σ_{j,o} Φout[x,j,o] = 1.

**What is explicit and what is learned.**
- **Explicit (geometry only, deterministic and noise-free):** Φin, Φout and W. They are computed by analytic exponential integration along rays through cells, using deterministic quasi-random rays. Monte Carlo is not used, because the noise-confound lesson of R6 applies here too. α_c is a known material constant.
- **Optional learned part:** a local closure ΔW on boundary-layer cells. It corrects the flat-collision-density error inside a cell and takes local E(3)-invariant geometric descriptors: signed distances to nearby boundary planes, the dihedral type (face / edge / corner), and dent curvature. It is parameterised as a symmetric nonnegative redistribution among nearby target cells, with row sums held fixed. That keeps conservation, reciprocity and positivity, and it needs only about 10³ parameters.
- **Nothing is indexed by the source port or by the boundary context.**

**Source and geometry conditioning.** A source enters only through Φin, which is exact. Boundary context enters through three geometric truncations:
- of the entry beam (Φin);
- of collision flights (W);
- of escape (Φout).

A corner source is therefore no more novel than an interior source: the same procedure computes all three fields.

**Angular representation.** There is no learned angular basis.
- Incoming-direction contrast arises *only* from the spatial distribution of first collisions, which Φin carries exactly. This follows from isotropic scattering (DERIVATION).
- The outgoing 64-bin structure is the exact escape geometry Φout.

The orientation-bias problem of R5 cannot arise.

**Transformation behaviour.**
- **Exact symmetries.** If the cell partition is invariant under the 16-element group (mirror-symmetric about both mid-planes; the square cross-section allows 90° maps) and P_g / Q_g are the exact port/bin permutations, then K = P_g K Q_gᵀ exactly. This is a DERIVATION.
- **Rigid motion:** K is unchanged and nothing is rebuilt.
- **Dent:** a deformed or cut-cell mesh is used, and only cells within about 5 mm of the dent change. The signed response change comes from the geometry, not from extrapolation.
- **Reciprocity:** V_x W[x,y] = V_y W[y,x]. Φout is the reversed-bin Φin up to area and étendue factors that depend on the bin convention. Both are DERIVATIONs, and the discrete form needs symmetric quadrature.

**Physical invariants and how they are guaranteed (DERIVATION).**
- **Linearity and zero output for zero input:** the operator is a product of fixed matrices.
- **Non-negativity:** every factor is nonnegative, and so is the Neumann series of (I − αW)⁻¹.
- **Passivity:** let e = Φout·1 = 1 − W·1 ≤ (I − αW)·1. Then

  ```
  Σ_{j,o} K = α Φin (I − αW)^{-1} e ≤ α Φin·1 = α d(i,a) ≤ d(i,a)
  ```

  This holds exactly in the discrete operator.
- **Channel structure:** the coefficients T_n do not depend on the channel, so RGB are three albedo filters of one geometric operator. A side consequence is that a single Monte Carlo run with collision-order tallies yields every channel, and every albedo.

**Why it should transfer to unseen sources (mechanism).** The only source-specific factor is computed exactly. The rest is a source-independent operator whose only approximation is spatial discretisation, plus the optional closure.
- **Discretisation** converges under refinement and is source-agnostic.
- **The closure** is local and depends on a handful of local geometry types (face, 90° edge, trihedral corner, dent curvature). Every face, edge and corner cell of the training geometry exhibits these types, whichever source illuminates it.

Generalisation across sources is therefore a property of the construction, not something learned (DERIVATION, conditional on discretisation convergence).

**Training supervision.** The base operator needs none. References serve three purposes:
- validating it;
- fitting ΔW, if needed, using residuals from about 10 or more sources;
- an exact single-scatter check: comparing T_0 = Φin Φout with the reference's first-order tally isolates any bin-convention mismatch before anything else.

### Mechanism B (alternative): canonical-context kernels with exact path-splitting composition

```
K[i,a,j,o,c] ≈ (Q_{g_i} ⊗ P_{g_i}) T_{κ(i)}[a, δ_{ij}, o, c]
               − Σ_{z∈Σ_far, ω} K[i,a,z,ω,c] · R_{κ}[z,ω → j,o,c]
```

- **Canonicalisation.** g_i is the group-plus-translation element that maps source i to its canonical representative.
- **Context.** κ(i) = (face type, d₁, d₂) are the distances to the nearest one or two side planes or rims, capped at r_c ≈ 18 mm.
- **Receiver offset.** δ_ij is the receiver offset in the canonical frame.
- **Parent kernels.** T_κ is the response in a canonical "parent" medium: an infinite slab, a half-slab or a quarter-slab.
- **Path-splitting term.** The second term is an exact first-crossing (Dyson-type) identity for truncation planes that the parent does not contain (DERIVATION). Split each parent path at its first crossing of a truncation plane: the part before the crossing is the body's own output on that plane, and the remainder is parent transport R from the plane. If the plane's re-crossing kernel has norm below 1, which is plausible in a 10 mm slab because top and bottom absorb quickly (INFERENCE), the coupled form (I + X)⁻¹ is a convergent series. In the slab, that kernel is block-Toeplitz along the edge, so an FFT diagonalises it.

**Learned versus explicit.**
- **Learned / tabulated:** T_κ as a smooth, nonnegative function of 1–2 continuous context parameters. Interpolation in d uses convex weights and has the known limit d → ∞ (the interior kernel). The kernels are stored in a normalised form (T/d) so that passivity holds per source bin.
- **Explicit:** the exact symmetry and canonicalisation step g_i, B, and the composition.

**Why it transfers.** Exact symmetry plus the measured translation equivalence (3a) let one supervised source represent every port in its class. Across d, prediction becomes interpolation, not extrapolation. Corners can be either supervised as classes or *composed* from edge kernels through path splitting, which is the key falsifiable claim.

**Supervision.** About one source per class, roughly 50 sources × 4 GPU-min ≈ 3.5 GPU-h per pose. With probing (several sources per packet separated by more than 30 mm), I estimate 2–3× fewer packets (INFERENCE).

**Complexity.**
- **Storage:** about 52 classes × 16 incoming bins × 128 near receivers × 64 outgoing bins × 3 channels ≈ 2·10⁷ floats (80 MB), plus a low-rank tail for red's 5.5 % beyond 128 ports.
- **Application:** about 1.5·10⁹ MACs, roughly a few ms.

**Weakness.** Dents are a continuous family, so the class catalogue does not close over them. B is best seen as the rest-pose baseline, or the near-field part of a hybrid.

## 3. Arguments against my preferred mechanism (A)

1. **Discretisation accuracy may not be affordable.** The mean free path (1 mm) is comparable to the cells, and grazing incoming and outgoing bins depend on structure within about 0.2 mm of the surface (the Milne boundary layer). Flat-source collision-probability methods are known to need regions of optical thickness below about 1, or linear/exponential sources.
   - Reaching `con_coarse` ≤ 0.25 and `abs_fine16` ≤ 0.15 at the edges and corners of a 3,840-port contract might need N ≫ 10⁵, which would push W past the memory and rebuild budget.
   - Red amplifies spatial errors by up to about 1/(1 − αλ) ≈ 38. Conservation is exact by construction, but the spatial redistribution is not.
2. **The exactness is narrow.**
   - With an anisotropic phase (g ≠ 0), the post-collision state is (x, ω): collision space grows by N_ω², and A turns into a discrete-ordinates solver.
   - Refractive (Fresnel) boundaries add an internal-reflection term and make the boundary layer harder.
   - Heterogeneous media are fine.

   This test domain is the method's best case, and success here says little about real materials.
3. **It largely dissolves the "learned K" premise.** If geometry gives the operator in about 1 s, the learned asset has almost no role. ReST-GI's value would then rest only on reuse across illumination changes at 10–30 ms per application, against path-traced re-rendering at "a few seconds" for a small workload. That margin is moderate, and it is not established at scene scale.
4. **Relation to an approach already tried.** A shares the first-collision anchor with the approach in the brief whose outcome was confounded (R6). If the true floor of that approach were not about 0.07, the cause might lie in conventions (bin weighting, tally definition) that A would also inherit. The single-scatter check exists to catch this.

## 1. What the measured outcomes do and do not establish

### 1.1 Physical facts and what follows from them

**Shared extinction.** σ_t = 1000 m⁻¹ in every channel (FACT). DERIVATION: every geometric quantity (ballistic attenuation, first-flight kernels, escape probabilities) is the same for R, G and B. Only the single-scattering albedo ϖ_c differs.

**Isotropic phase function (FACT).** DERIVATION: each collision erases all directional memory. So for any path with at least one collision:
- the dependence on the incoming bin `a` enters only through where the first collision happens;
- the dependence on the outgoing bin `o` enters only through the flight from the last collision to the exit.

Both are pure ballistic geometry. Every collision-to-collision step is a scalar, direction-free transfer. This is the main structural fact the representation should use.

**Diffusion scales (DERIVATION, diffusion approximation; not a measurement).**
- Absorption σ_a = σ_t(1 − ϖ) = 1 / 10 / 100 m⁻¹ (R / G / B).
- Diffusion length L = 1/√(3σ_aσ_t) = 18.3 / 5.8 / 1.8 mm.
- The 10 mm slab adds a leakage mode B ≈ π/(10 + 2·0.71) mm ≈ 0.275 mm⁻¹, using the Milne extrapolation length of about 0.71 mfp.
- Lateral decay constant κ = √(B² + L⁻²) ≈ 0.28 / 0.33 / 0.61 mm⁻¹, which gives decay lengths of about 3.5 / 3.1 / 1.6 mm.

It also agrees with the context-equivalence fact: the influence of a second boundary at 18.75 mm is about e^(−5) in red, near the 0.002 noise level. So the measured locality is what one slowest slab mode plus a boundary layer of a few mfp would produce. The far field is simple. The hard part is the near-boundary layer.

## 2. Mechanisms

### M1 (preferred): collision-factorized volumetric operator (CFV)

#### Operator (DERIVATION: exact in the continuum for a homogeneous, isotropic-scattering, index-matched medium)

```
K[i,a,j,o,c] = Σ_{x,y ∈ V} F1[i,a ; x] · Γ_c[x, y] · E_c[y ; j,o]

Γ_c = (I − ϖ_c P_V)^(-1)
E_c = ϖ_c · Ê
```

Here x and y are volume cells.

- **F1[i,a ; x]** is the first-collision probability in cell x for unit power entering port i in bin a:

  ∫_{port i} ∫_{bin a} ∫_{s: ray in V} σ_t e^(−σ_t s) 1[x(s) ∈ cell x] ds dω dA, with the port-frame weighting of the contract.

  It is exact geometry with no learning. It is channel-independent. FACT/DERIVATION: Σ_x F1[i,a ; x] = 1 − β(i,a) = d(i,a), so the collided budget is reproduced exactly, and the ballistic part B stays separate by construction.
- **P_V[x, y]** is the first-flight collision kernel inside V:

  σ_t e^(−σ_t|x−y|) / (4π|x−y|²) · vis_V(x, y), averaged over the cells.

  It is the Peierls kernel. It is channel-independent and symmetric under volume weighting. For a convex body, vis_V = 1 and P_V is the free-space convolution kernel P_∞ masked to V. That is exact, not an approximation.
- **Ê[y ; j,o]** is the probability that a collision at y emits isotropically (1/4π) into a direction in bin o, in port j's frame, and leaves V uncollided through port j. It is the reverse of F1: by reciprocity, Ê equals F1 for a reverse beam entering j along −o, up to the radiance and cosine weights the contract specifies. It is exact geometry.

#### Explicit and learned parts

| Part | Status |
|---|---|
| F1, Ê, P_V, vis_V, cell masks, port/bin frames | Explicit geometry; recomputed from the current shape; never learned |
| ϖ_c | Material constant |
| Γ_c | Computed by a linear solve; no learning required |

Learning is optional and only accelerates the numerics:
- **(a) Sub-cell closure.** A local correction to P_V in boundary-cut cells, so that 1–2 mm cells can be used. Its inputs are the signed distances and normals of up to 3 boundary planes, plus dent curvature, all in a gauge-fixed local frame. It is trained on deterministic fine-grid solutions of canonical patches: half-slab, 90° wedge, octant corner, and a family of curved dents. These cover all chart types exhaustively and cheaply, with no Monte Carlo noise.
- **(b) Low-rank compression of the smooth part of Γ.** This only buys speed.

#### Conditioning and transformation behaviour

- There are no source, receiver or position features. The source enters only as the right-hand side through F1, and the receiver only through Ê.
- **Rigid motion:** everything lives in body coordinates, so the operator is exactly invariant.
- **The 16 rest symmetries:** use cubic cells aligned with the box. For example, 0.5 mm cells give 200 × 200 × 20, a lattice that D4h maps onto itself. Then g acts by permutation on cells, ports and bins, and K is exactly equivariant. This holds to float precision if the quadrature points are D4h-symmetric (DERIVATION).
- **Dents:** recompute F1 rows for ports on or near the dent, Ê for the cells and ports within about 7 mm of it, the mask, and the visibility correction ΔP_vis for segments crossing the air cavity. Port frames can follow any convention, because bins are integrated in whatever frame the contract defines. The non-symmetric dent frames are irrelevant here.

#### Physical invariants (DERIVATION)

- **Linearity, and zero output for zero input:** structural, provided Γ is applied as a fixed linear map. That means a direct factorisation or a fixed iteration count with fixed coefficients. A truncated Krylov method (CG) depends on the right-hand side and is not exactly linear.
- **Non-negativity:** F1, Ê, P ≥ 0, and (I − ϖP) is a nonsingular M-matrix, so its inverse is ≥ 0. Neumann or Jacobi iterates started at 0 stay non-negative and increase monotonically.
- **Passivity:** make the discrete chain substochastic. For each cell, Σ_y P_V[x, y] + esc(x) ≤ 1, where esc is computed geometrically (scale rows down if quadrature overshoots). Total output is then d(i,a) minus absorbed energy minus truncation, which is at most d(i,a). Truncation only removes energy.
- **Correct source/receiver/bin correspondence:** every index is a computed physical integral.
- **Material-domain ownership:** collision chains never leave V. Any segment through air, including the dent cavity, is an exit, and re-entry belongs to C.
- **Reciprocity:** comes for free from Γ being symmetric and Ê being the reverse of F1.

#### Why it should transfer to unseen sources (the mechanism)

Nothing in the operator is indexed by a source.
- A corner source differs from a training source only in its F1 column: an exact ray integral near the corner.
- The corner's boundary-layer physics lives in P_V and vis_V near the corner: an exact kernel restricted to the true geometry.
- The angular contrast that defeated shared profiles is reproduced by F1 and Ê exactly. On the outgoing side this extends the brief's own observation that first-interaction anchoring is exact.

What remains is discretisation error. It is checkable and convergent, and it does not depend on the supervision population. The population limit disappears because the deterministic solver generates its own noise-free data for any source.

HYPOTHESIS: at 0.5 mm cells, with sub-cell exact integration of F1, Ê and P, the discretisation error is below the gates (abs_coarse ≤ 0.10, con_coarse ≤ 0.25) on all strata.

#### Supervision

None for the operator. Monte Carlo references are used only to validate it. A learned closure, if used, is trained on deterministic fine-grid canonical patches (0.25 mm), with every chart type covered by construction.

#### Complexity

P = 3,840 ports, 16 incoming and 64 outgoing bins, RGB. Uniform 0.5 mm cells give N_v = 800,000. Dense K would be 3,840·16 × 3,840·64·3 ≈ 4.5e10 entries, about 181 GB in fp32, so it must be applied as an operator and never materialised (DERIVATION).

### M2 (cheaper runtime layer): gauge-fixed context atlas plus a steerable dent interpolator

#### Operator

```

             + K_far[i,a,j,o,c]
```

- κ(i) is the context class of source i (about 52 classes at rest).

- K_far is the coarse part of M1 (coarse Γ plus exact Ê), or a single-slab-mode diffusion kernel.

**Explicit:** the class map, the gauge elements, the bin permutations (already verified exact), and K_far.

**Tabulated:** the table A for each class.

- inputs: radial offset relative to the dent radius, depth, radius, and the receiver offset;
- angular state held internally in a steerable basis (circular harmonics in azimuth × elevation profiles), then projected onto the contract's bins by exact precomputed bin-integral matrices.

Reason: the 4×4 and 8×8 square-to-disk bins carry only D4. An arbitrary rotation about the dent axis does not permute bins. The network is trained on M1-generated dent families: dense, noise-free, cheap.

- **At rest, nothing is extrapolated.** Every port maps to one of about 52 classes, and every class is tabulated (DERIVATION, conditional on the cutoff R_c). Exact symmetry is structural: the same table entry is read through a permutation.

- Linear by construction.
- Non-negative and passive if each table column is stored as M1 output, or renormalised against d(i,a) by an input-independent scale.
- The learned dent part should output non-negative coefficients, with each source column normalised by a geometry-only factor so that the output total stays ≤ d. Linearity is kept because the factor does not depend on the input.

## 3. Serious arguments against my own preference (M1)

1. **It largely gives up on a learned K.** M1 is a deterministic volumetric transport solver, close to the zonal method. If ReST-GI's contribution is meant to be a learned, reusable K, M1 answers the question by saying very little should be learned, at least for this material class.
   - Its runtime advantage is not established. At about 5–20 ms per application, a 10–30 iteration GI solve costs 0.1–0.6 s. Matched-quality path tracing of a small medium workload takes "a few seconds" (FACT).
   - The reuse advantage could be marginal (INFERENCE).
2. **It depends on the isotropic phase function.** With an anisotropic phase function (for example Henyey–Greenstein with g ≠ 0), the collision state needs a direction, Γ becomes Γ(x,ω; y,ω′), and storage and solve cost grow by the angular resolution, as in discrete ordinates. A Fresnel (index-mismatched) boundary adds an angular internal-reflection state at the boundary. Those materials would lose most of the clean scalar structure.

If the rest and dented poses discretise differently (different cut cells, different visibility sets), the bias may not cancel, and the gates on signed-change relative error (≤ 0.35) and correlation (≥ 0.90) could fail even when the absolute errors pass. Mitigation: keep one lattice, integrate exactly at the boundary, and refine near the dent. This is a HYPOTHESIS that needs testing.
5. **Tension between linearity, positivity and speed.** Fast solvers (CG, Chebyshev, aggressive multigrid) are either nonlinear when truncated, or are linear polynomials whose coefficients can be negative. The guaranteed-safe options (Neumann iteration, M-matrix direct inverses) are slower or memory-hungry. The two-grid compromise is unproven on positivity in every channel.
6. **The quadrature confound may repeat.** The brief's own post hoc finding (q = 1,024 single-scatter noise confounding an angular floor) shows that "exact geometric operators" are only as good as their quadrature. The F1/Ê bin integrals near grazing directions and edges could be under-resolved.

Against M2: the atlas is exact only for this rest box and depends on the cutoff R_c, which was validated on near-field and fine16 metrics only. It does not carry over to general shapes, where every port is its own context. In the general case M2 reduces to M1 plus a learned interpolator, with the usual coverage risk.

## 2. Mechanisms

### 2.0 Key derivation (DERIVATION, holds for the brief's stated physics)

Assumptions, all stated in the brief: homogeneous medium, isotropic phase function, index-matched boundary (no Fresnel, no internal reflection), and σ_t equal in all three channels.

Index conventions:
- u, v are volume cells.
- ψ is the collision density.
- Every collision re-emits a fraction ω_c of its photons uniformly over 4π, so direction memory is lost entirely at the first collision.

The collided response therefore factorises exactly:

```
S[i,a,j,o,c] = ω_c · Σ_{u,v} E[j,o ; u] · G_c[u,v] · F[v ; i,a]

F[v ; i,a]  = (1/Φ_ia) ∫_{port i} ∫_{bin a} |cosθ| ∫_0^{s_exit} σ_t e^{−σ_t s} 1[x+sω ∈ v] ds dω dA
              (first-collision deposit; geometry only; channel-independent)
E[j,o ; u]  = (1/|u|) ∫_u (1/4π) ∫_{ω∈bin o of port j} e^{−σ_t s(y,ω)} 1[ray from y exits through port j, chord ⊂ medium] dω dy
              (last-flight escape; geometry only; channel-independent)
𝒦[u,v]      = (1/|v|) ∫_u ∫_v σ_t e^{−σ_t r}/(4π r²) · 1[segment ⊂ medium] dx dy
              (collision-to-next-collision; scalar; geometry only)
G_c         = (I − ω_c 𝒦)^{-1}      (scalar volume Green's operator; only channel dependence is ω_c)
```

Consequences:
- All incoming-direction and outgoing-direction structure lives in F and E. Both are exact ray integrals in the true geometry and the true per-port frames.
- The only operator that is not a single ray integral is G_c. It is *scalar and source-independent*.
- There is nothing angular to learn.

This sharpens outcome 6 (DERIVATION): for this medium, the "angular redistribution" after the first collision is exactly uniform. Any learned angular redistribution is learning a geometric quantity that can be computed exactly.

### Mechanism A (preferred): exact-flight / scalar-collision operator with a two-grid solve. No learning is required for correctness.

**Explicit (exact, geometry-only):**
- F, E and 𝒦 are computed from the current geometry and port frames by analytic or quadrature ray integration.
- Each cell's quadrature rays are split exactly into "exits through (j,o)" and "collides in cell u". This gives Σ_{j,o} E[·;v] + Σ_u |u|𝒦[u,v] = 1 per cell by construction.
- The ballistic part is consistent by construction: Σ_v F[v;i,a] = d(i,a) = 1 − β(i,a). The same ray march gives B.

**Runtime solve of G_c (a solver, not a fitted model):**
- Fine grid: uniform h = 0.5 mm, 200×200×20 = 8.0·10⁵ cells, centred so that the grid is invariant under D4h.
- For a convex body, 1[segment ⊂ medium] = indicator(x)·indicator(y) exactly (DERIVATION: a convex set contains every chord between its points). 𝒦 is then a masked FFT convolution with the truncated kernel e^{−σ_t r}/(4πr²), with r_max ≈ 10 mm (e^{−10} ≈ 4.5·10⁻⁵).
- For the dented (non-convex) top, chords that leave through the dent are removed by a sparse near-field correction. This covers pairs within about 6 mm near the dent surface and is recomputed at rebuild. Longer chords carry less than e^{−6}·(1/4πr²), which is negligible (INFERENCE).
- Tail acceleration uses diffusion-synthetic-acceleration-style two-grid cycles: a fine Neumann step plus a coarse Galerkin correction P(I − ω R𝒦P)^{-1}R.
  - The coarse grid is port-aligned: 2.5 mm laterally with about 8 graded depth layers, ≈ 1.3–1.6·10⁴ cells per channel.
  - The coarse system is solved by preconditioned CG. It is symmetric in the volume-weighted inner product.
  - The solve is warm-started from the previous GI iteration.

**Optional learning, deliberately placed where an error costs only time:**
- A learned prolongation or preconditioner (for example a boundary-layer, Milne-type shape function per local geometry type) may speed the cycles up.
- At convergence the answer is the discretised physics whatever the learned part is.
- No source conditioning exists anywhere.

**How the physical invariants are guaranteed:**

| Property | Guarantee |

| Linearity / zero output for zero input | The operator is a product of linear maps. |

  - G_c is the resolvent of the in-medium flight kernel.
- E_in and X are pure geometry plus σ_t, and are identical for all channels. Channels differ only through the scalar c_c inside G.
- Consequence (DERIVATION): every dependence on the incoming bin a, including all of `con_*`, enters only through E_in. The outgoing-bin dependence enters only through X. Both are explicit line integrals with known values.

**Diffusion lengths (DERIVATION, using L = √(D/σ_a), D = 1/(3σ_t)).** L ≈ 18.3 / 5.8 / 1.8 mm for R / G / B.
- This agrees with the measured energy locality: 5.5 / 3.2 / 0.1 % lands beyond about 15 mm.
- The red signal is slab-confined and spreads sideways further than the slab is thick (10 mm). Its sensitivity to side faces therefore reaches about 2L.

**Reading of the outcomes:**

- **FACT:** Every learned direct map from (i,a) to (j,o) fits its supervised sources. None meets the gates on independent boundary sources.
  - **INFERENCE:** The boundary-context dependence in these models comes from 4–16 examples. Corners, vertical edges and side rims plausibly have zero supervised members.
  - **DERIVATION:** A context class with no supervised members can only be predicted from built-in structure, not by interpolation.
  - **Not established:** that the cause is capacity, the optimiser or the features.
- **FACT:** Geometry-relative features fixed INT (0.12) but not BND_FAR (0.67 / 0.86).
  - **INFERENCE:** Invariance helps where the supervised contexts span the test contexts. It does not create missing context classes.
- **FACT:** Shifting the coordinates changes predictions by 10–23 %, and symmetric references disagree by 12–141 %.
  - **INFERENCE:** The models are not equivariant by construction.
  - The penalty acted only on angle-integrated responses, so the directional part was unconstrained. Its failure to help is therefore weak evidence.
- **FACT:** The per-source oracle with a shared, fixed angular basis reaches about 0.27 `con_coarse` with 8 profiles. Its error is 1.26–1.41× worse on symmetry images, and about 1.15× after refitting on the orbit.
  - **INFERENCE:** A global angular dictionary in per-port frames is (a) orientation-biased and (b) too small for boundary contexts.
  - **HYPOTHESIS:** Near edges, X truncates the in-medium chord of grazing backward rays. This gives each context its own sharp angular features that a shared basis cannot express compactly.
  - **Not established:** that any angular basis would fail.
- **FACT, post hoc:** The first-interaction-anchored factorisation was exact. Its 0.38–0.45 azimuthal floors were confounded by quadrature noise; noise-free estimates are about 0.07.
  - **INFERENCE:** This is the one measured hint that explicit geometric factors work. The "learned redistribution" was not shown to be necessary or sufficient.
- **FACT:** A symmetric context whose second-nearest boundary moves from 18.75 mm to 41–49 mm gives an identical near field.
  - **INFERENCE:** The near field depends on local geometry within a few mean free paths. This fits explicit, locally built operators.
  - **Not established:** the far-field red behaviour, which was not reported.
- **FACT:** Transfer peaks early in training and then degrades.
  - **INFERENCE:** The model memorises per-source specifics. The data cannot say which specifics.

## 2. Mechanisms

### Mechanism 1 (preferred): explicit Peierls / collision-probability factorisation

Every factor is geometric, there is no per-source supervision, and learning is optional and only for speed.

**Operator.** Partition the medium into V cells x, graded toward every face.

- **E_in[x; i,a]** = ∫_port ∫_bin (cosθ / norm) ∫ σ_t e^{−σ_t s} 1[r(s)∈x] ds.
  - Computed with the same rays as the ballistic operator B, so Σ_x E_in = 1 − β(i,a) = d(i,a) holds to floating-point accuracy.
- **T0[x, x']** = probability that an isotropic scatter, uniform in x', has its next collision in x.
  - Only in-medium segments count. In a dented pose, a segment that leaves through the cavity is C's re-entry and is excluded.
- **X[(j,o); x']** = probability that this scatter exits through port j into bin o with no further collision.
- **Column partition of unity, by construction:** 1ᵀT0 + 1ᵀX = 1ᵀ.
  - Each sampled (point, direction) in x' deposits exponential segment integrals and then gives its remaining mass to its exit (j,o).
  - So the identity holds for any quadrature count.
- **Full operator:**

  K_c[j,o; i,a] = c_c · S1[j,o; i,a] + Σ_{x,x'} X[j,o;x] · c_c² · [T0 (I − c_c T0)^{-1}]_{x x'} · E_in[x'; i,a]

  - S1 is the exact single-scatter term, X·E_in at fine quadrature. It is sparse and holds most of the near field.
  - The multiple-scatter remainder goes through the cells.
- **Explicit vs learned:** everything is explicit. The only inputs are σ_t, c_c, the surface mesh and the port/bin definitions.

**Invariants, and how they are guaranteed (DERIVATION).**
- **Linearity, zero-in/zero-out, non-negativity:** all factors are ≥ 0 and the Neumann series is ≥ 0.
- **Passivity:**
  - Let ψ = (I − cT0)^{-1} q with q = E_in u.
  - Then 1ᵀψ = 1ᵀq + c(1ᵀψ − p_escᵀψ), so the output c·p_escᵀψ = d − (1−c)1ᵀψ ≤ d(i,a), exactly.
  - Passivity therefore follows from the column partition alone and needs no penalty.
- **Port/bin correspondence:** this is the definition of X and E_in, not a learned association.
- **Exact rest symmetries:**
  - Build the cell grid and quadrature sets on a 1/16 fundamental domain and replicate them.
  - K then commutes with all 16 operations to floating-point accuracy, including the brief's exact bin permutations.
  - The volume field is scalar and frame-free, so only E_in and X touch port frames. Both are computed per port in whatever frame convention is in use.
- **Reciprocity:** T0[x,x']·|x'| = T0[x',x]·|x| holds analytically for a homogeneous medium. It can be enforced by computing each pair's integral once.
- **Ownership:** K contains in-medium segments only. The ballistic part B is separate and exactly complementary through d.

**Conditioning and geometry change.**
- Source, receiver and geometry enter only through ray/segment integrals against the current surface.
- A dent is handled by re-meshing the cells near the dent. Cut or conforming cells within about 6 mm of the changed surface are recomputed: their E_in and X rows and their T0 rows/columns, including in-medium visibility for the now non-convex top.
- The signed response change is then a discretisation-accurate physical difference, not a regression output.
- Rigid motion leaves K invariant.

**Why it should transfer to unseen sources (mechanism, not capacity).**
- No source is supervised, so the gap between supervised and unsupervised sources is zero by construction.
- Boundary context (corner, vertical edge, rim) enters only through where kernel mass escapes. Escape is an exact geometric chord computation, identical in form for every context class.
- The residual error is spatial/angular discretisation error. It should shrink under local refinement at every context, and it is verifiable against the existing references.
- The 16-source MC set becomes validation data, not training data.

**Complexity (DERIVATION from stated assumptions; throughput figures are SPECULATION).**

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Cells V | ≈ 32k: 40×40 lateral × about 16 depth layers graded 0.25 → 1.5 mm, plus normal-direction refinement next to the side faces |
| E_in | 61,440 columns × about 20 nnz ≈ 1.2M |
| X | 245,760 rows × about 30 nnz ≈ 7.4M |
| T0 near field | Cells within about 5 mm, about 300 nnz per column ≈ 1e7 |
| T0 far field | Two-level aggregation on about 800 super-cells, dense, 0.64M. Mass must not be truncated: dropping e^{−5} per flight compounds over about 100 collisions and biases red |
| S1 | 61,440 × about 9 ports × 64 bins ≈ 3.5e7, scaled by c per channel |
| Storage | About 5e7 values ≈ 0.3–0.4 GB, mostly channel-independent. Fits 12 GB comfortably |
| Spectral radius of T0 | About 0.975 from slab diffusion: B ≈ π / (10 + 2·0.71) mm⁻¹ and λ ≈ 1 − B²/3 |
| Red iterations | Plain Neumann at 1e-3 needs about 260. D^{1/2}-symmetrised CG needs about 30–60, but its iterates are not monotone and positive only at convergence. Diffusion-synthetic acceleration needs about 10–20 |
| Cost per K application | About 1e9–2e9 multiply-adds for RGB, i.e. roughly 10–40 ms on one GPU. About 10× slower than the reported ms-scale learned operator |
| Alternative: compressed precompute | Use Mechanism 1 to build an H-matrix of the boundary-to-boundary K: dense near field within about 25 ports, low-rank far field. Size about 1e8–1.5e8 per channel; application about 2–5 ms |
| Alternative: inside the GI solve | Merge the inner iteration with the outer K∘C iteration and warm-start across frames |
| Dent rebuild | About 25 % of the top columns change, giving about 1e7 segment integrals, roughly ≤ 0.1–0.3 s. Comparable to the measured 0.1–0.5 s |

**Training supervision.** None is required. Optionally, add a small learned correction ΔK only if discretisation error exceeds the gates after one refinement level. If one is added, supervise it with MC tallies of the interior collision density, which are cheap and much denser per photon than exit tensors, rather than with boundary exits alone.

### Mechanism 2 (fallback, learnable, for media where G has no closed form)

Use when the phase function is anisotropic, the boundary has Fresnel reflection, or the material is measured.

- **Operator:** K = c·S1 + Σ X[j,o;x] · Ĝ_θ,c(x, x') · E_in[x';i,a], with E_in and X still explicit.
- **Learned part:** only a nonnegative scalar Green's function. In this domain the in-medium kernel is analytic, so the image construction below plus learning applies to the anisotropic or Fresnel variants (an extrapolated boundary plane assumes Fresnel-type boundary conditions).
  - Ĝ_θ(x,x') = Σ_{m ∈ images} s_m · G_∞(|x − R_m x'|; c) · exp(h_θ(φ(x), φ(x'), r)).
  - G_∞ is the analytic infinite-medium resolvent: a 1-D radial function of r/mfp and c.
  - The images R_m are reflections across extrapolated planes of the faces within about 2L: one for a face, three for an edge, seven for a corner, and a series for the slab pair.
  - h_θ is a small residual.
  - φ holds intrinsic features only: signed distances to the nearest faces, the dihedral type, and the in-plane offset in a canonical local frame. It contains no absolute coordinates.
- **Transfer argument (HYPOTHESIS):**
  - By the measured locality FACT, every context of a box-like body decomposes into a finite set of canonical local contexts (half-space, 90° wedge, triple corner, slab).
  - Supervise h_θ with volume point sources in those canonical contexts. That covers every boundary class by construction, rather than through the training-source positions.
  - Equivariance is exact because φ is invariant and the images are built geometrically.
- **Passivity:** not automatic. It requires renormalising Ĝ columns against the escape balance, i.e. a projection.

## 3. Arguments against my preferred mechanism

1. **It dissolves the "learned K" thesis.** Mechanism 1 is a deterministic collision-probability transport solver. For this test domain the honest answer to "what should K learn?" may be "nothing". Learning would be justified only for speed (preconditioners, compressed far field) or for materials outside this case.
   - The novelty burden then moves entirely to the K/C dynamic decomposition.
   - The cost comparison is against path tracing at a few seconds for this workload. My estimated 10–40 ms per application is not obviously enough once precompute and rebuild are counted (SPECULATION until measured).
2. **The scalar factorisation is fragile outside this regime.**
   - Fresnel boundaries add internal-reflection kernels: about 40–60 % diffuse internal reflection at n ≈ 1.4–1.5 (INFERENCE from standard values), which lengthens paths a lot.
   - Anisotropic phase (g ≈ 0.8–0.9) needs angularly resolved cells, S_N or P_N, multiplying memory and cost by about 16–100.
   - Optically denser media (mfp ≪ 1 mm) force boundary-layer cells far below the port size, so V grows with volume/h³.
   - The 2026 brief's slab is close to the best case for this mechanism.
3. **The discretisation bias is systematic, not random.**
   - Flat-source collision-probability methods carry known boundary-layer errors.
   - Edges and corners have 3-D boundary layers, which is exactly where the current failures sit.
   - If refinement converges slowly there, the mechanism inherits the same corner problem in deterministic form.
4. **Positivity under fast solvers.** CG and Chebyshev iterates are not monotone. Strict positivity holds only for Neumann/Jacobi or at convergence, so a fast solver may need a clip that breaks exact passivity bookkeeping.

## 4. Closest prior work

- **Collision-probability and interface-current methods (reactor physics).** These are decades-old textbook material: Peierls integral equation, cell-to-cell first-flight probabilities, escape probabilities to boundary surfaces, and diffusion-synthetic acceleration (Alcouffe, 1977). Mechanism 1 is essentially this, applied with graphics port/bin interfaces. **Not new.**
- **Response-matrix / coarse-mesh transport, COMET.** S. W. Mosher and F. Rahnema, "The incident flux response expansion method for heterogeneous coarse mesh transport problems", *Transport Theory and Statistical Physics* 35 (2006) 55–86.
  - Responses of local coarse meshes to expansions of boundary incident flux in space and angle are precomputed stochastically and coupled through interfaces by sweeps.
  - This is the closest conceptual precedent for the K/C split itself. It is not learned and is aimed at reactor eigenvalue problems.
- **Modular Radiance Transfer.** Loos, Antani, Mitchell, Nowrouzezahrai, Jarosz and Sloan, *ACM TOG* 30(6), SIGGRAPH Asia 2011. Transport is precomputed on reusable proxy modules and coupled at runtime: the graphics analogue of reusable intrinsic blocks plus runtime coupling, for coarse indirect light.
- **Analytic BSSRDFs with explicit beam integration:**
  - Jensen et al. 2001: dipole.
  - Donner & Jensen 2005: multipole images for thin slabs. This is the image series behind Mechanism 2.
  - d'Eon & Irving 2011: quantized diffusion.
  - Habel, Christensen & Jarosz 2013: photon beam diffusion, which integrates along the incident beam like E_in.
  - Frisvad et al. 2014: directional dipole.
  - Li, Pellacini & Torrance 2005: hybrid of exact single scattering and diffusion.
- **Learned, geometry-adaptive SSS.** Vicini, Koltun and Jakob, "A Learned Shape-Adaptive Subsurface Scattering Model", *ACM TOG* 38(4), SIGGRAPH 2019.
  - A conditional VAE, trained on a volumetric path tracer, samples exit positions given a local polynomial shape descriptor.
  - It is a sampling model inside MC, not a linear port operator. Its transfer comes from local shape conditioning, which is close in spirit to Mechanism 2's canonical-context features.
  - Kallweit et al. 2017, "Deep Scattering" (clouds), uses local hierarchical density stencils, also geometry-relative.
- **Precomputed transport for translucency.**
  - Sloan et al. 2003, clustered-PCA PRT including subsurface scattering.
  - R. Wang, Tran and Luebke 2005, all-frequency relighting of translucent objects (single plus multiple scattering, compressed).
  - Wang et al. 2008, polygrid diffusion for heterogeneous translucency; Arbree, Walter and Bala 2011, FEM.
- **Learning Green's functions from few forcings.**
  - Boullé & Townsend (e.g. "Learning elliptic PDEs with randomized linear algebra", FoCM 2023) show that hierarchical low-rank Green's functions can be recovered from a small number of random forcings.
  - This has a direct supervision implication: random multiplexed boundary illumination may be far more informative per photon than single-port packets.
  - The same idea appears in light-transport acquisition: Peers et al. 2009, compressive light transport sensing; Wang et al. 2009, kernel Nyström; O'Toole & Kutulakos 2010, optical Krylov.
  - I give these identities from memory, with moderate confidence on exact titles and years.
- **What might plausibly be new (INFERENCE):**
  - An exactly passive, exactly D4h-equivariant first-exit port/bin operator, built from collision-probability factors with exact ballistic complementarity, used as the reusable K inside a dynamic graphics GI split.
  - Local rebuild of that operator under surface deformation.
  - Neither the factorisation nor the domain decomposition is new.

## 5. Minimal falsification experiment

All steps use existing references only. No training and no new MC beyond step 0.

0. **H-function check.** Build the 1-D graded depth discretisation for a half-space. If the discrete operator misses the Chandrasekhar H-function albedo for normal and grazing incidence by more than about 1 % at the planned grading, the discretisation fails before any slab test.
1. **Predeclared slab check at rest.**
   - Before looking at the references, predeclare the grid (h, depth grading, far-field level) and a refinement h/2.
   - Build K for the rest slab.
   - Evaluate the existing gates on (a) S16-R, all 16 sources, and (b) the independent INT / BND_NEAR / BND_FAR population, which is never used for anything else.
   - Check the 16 symmetry operations to floating-point accuracy and passivity column by column.
2. **Falsification rules for Mechanism 1 (predeclared).** It is falsified for this domain if either:
   - BND_FAR `con_coarse` > 0.25 or `abs_coarse` > 0.10 at level h/2; or
   - the BND_FAR error shrinks by less than half of its INT counterpart's relative reduction under h → h/2, i.e. corner and edge errors that do not converge.

   The transfer claim specifically predicts no significant difference between supervised-type and BND_FAR error after refinement.
3. **Deformation.** Use one existing dented pose with local rebuild. The signed change must have relative error ≤ 0.35 and correlation ≥ 0.90 per channel, including blue.
4. **Cost.** Report the wall time per K application, and per H-compressed application, against the measured path-traced comparator. A failure here does not falsify the physics; it is recorded separately.
5. **Mechanism 2.** Fit h_θ only on half-space and slab point sources, then test on edge and corner sources. If corner `con_coarse` exceeds 0.25 while face contexts pass, the canonical-context decomposition does not transfer.
