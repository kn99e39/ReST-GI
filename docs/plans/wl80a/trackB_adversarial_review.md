## Objections

**1. MAJOR, close to FATAL as written: the split form breaks the passivity and conservation identity it claims.** (DERIVATION, with numbers worked by hand)
- The passivity proof (ω eᵀψ = d − (1−ω)1ᵀψ ≤ d) needs one cell-level partition 1ᵀW + 1ᵀΦout = 1 applied to the same first-collision distribution.
- The split form does not do that:
  - S1 escapes first collisions with the exact point distribution.
  - The remainder continues them with the flat-cell W (1ᵀW_x = 1 − P̄_esc,x).
- Point escape probability at depth z is E₂(z)/2. For a beam with direction cosine μ, ∫(e^{−z/μ}/μ)E₂(z)/2 dz = [1 − μ ln(1+1/μ)]/2. That gives 0.424 at μ = 0.05 and 0.380 at μ = 0.1.
- The flat average over a 0.5 mm top cell is (½ − E₃(0.5))/h·½ ≈ 0.278.
- So the remainder overcounts continuing first collisions by +0.146 and +0.102 of q.
- For red (ω ≈ 1, remainder escape about 0.97), the total is ΣK ≈ 1.08–1.12·d for near-horizon directions. That breaches the hard contract ΣK ≤ d.
- Bin-averaged over the outer incoming ring (μ from 0 to 0.87), I estimate the excess at about 1–5 % of d at h = 0.5 mm (INFERENCE). It is first order in h: at h = 0.25 mm the μ = 0.05 excess halves to 0.073.
- The unsplit form restores exact passivity but puts the same flat-source error into S1, where it distorts the incident-angle contrast directly.
- So, as specified, the candidate cannot have both exact passivity and accurate grazing contrast.
- Fix: a consistent first flight. Deposit the second collision from the fine first-collision quadrature points (point-to-cell W₁), so that per point P_esc + 1ᵀW₁ = 1. Use a symmetric last-flight estimator on output. Without that, the "exact at convergence" claim is false at every finite h.

**2. MAJOR: the per-cell partition of unity is claimed "by construction" but contradicts the specified build.** (DERIVATION / INFERENCE)
- In option (U), W comes from an analytic or FFT kernel with an analytic near field, and Φout from separate reverse beams. These are not the same sampled emissions, so 1ᵀW + 1ᵀΦout = 1 holds only to quadrature and kernel accuracy.
- Sensitivity:
  - For red, a per-collision gain or leak ε becomes a total-energy error of about ε times the mean collision count, up to about 38ε in the fundamental mode. So ε = 1e-3 gives up to about 4 %.
  - The candidate's own statement that red amplifies redistribution error is misdirected. What red amplifies is conservation error.
  - Redistribution bias from cell re-randomisation inflates the diffusion coefficient by about (h²/12)/(2/3) = h²/8. That is about 3 % at h = 0.5 mm, giving about 1.5 % in diffusion length. This is minor.
- Mitigation: define the escape total as 1 − 1ᵀW and normalise Φout's port and bin distribution to it. The renormalisation bias must then be measured.

**3. MAJOR: information loss below cell scale persists for grazing incidence at any finite h.** (DERIVATION) This is the requested counterexample, given in full below. Bins near the horizon on top, side and rim faces put their first collisions within 0.01–0.1 mm of the surface, far below h. The remainder is blind to that depth.

**4. MAJOR: the dent is handled by a binary re-mask (a staircase surface), inconsistent with exact-mesh F_in and Φout, and the learned closure cannot repair the dominant error.** (DERIVATION / INFERENCE)
- The dent wall slopes up to about 30°. A 0.5 mm staircase misrepresents surface area and normals at the 0.5 mfp scale, which is exactly where escape is decided.
- F_in and Φout are computed against the true mesh but deposit into cut cells that the mask treats as fully in or fully out. That breaks complementarity and the partition locally.
- The rest top surface is lattice-aligned and has no cut cells; the dented pose has them. The discretisation bias therefore does not cancel in the signed change, and it sits exactly where the signed change lives. This threatens the 0.35 / 0.90 deformation gate.
- The optional ΔW keeps row sums fixed, so it cannot correct the escape fraction, which is the first-order cut-cell error.
- "Symmetric" ΔW also conflicts with reciprocity for unequal cut volumes, which is volume-weighted (V_x W_xy = V_y W_yx).
- Needed: volume-fraction (cut-cell) integration of W, which breaks the FFT translation invariance near the dent.
- Minor note: the 6 mm chord-correction band is far larger than needed. For a dent with curvature radius about 36 mm, the sagitta of a 3 mm chord is about 0.03 mm, so blocked chords matter only at a sharp rim or kink.

**5. MAJOR: edges, corners and side rims, the BND_FAR strata, compound the flat-source boundary-layer error, and convergence there may be unaffordable.** (INFERENCE / HYPOTHESIS)
- Corner and edge cells escape through two or three faces, so the within-cell variation in escape probability is about two to three times the single-face case of objection 1.
- The error converges only at first order in h.
- At h = 0.25 mm uniform: 6.4e6 cells, an 800×800×80 FFT grid (about 0.4 GB per complex array), and F_in about 2e8 nnz plus Φout about 8e8 nnz. That exceeds 12 GB unless computed on the fly.
- The graded option (G) loses the FFT.
- So the "finest affordable level" may be the level where the BND_FAR error is still about first order in h.

**6. MAJOR (methodological): the falsifier has an escape clause.**
- Criterion 3(a) requires both "exceeds the gate" and "the error has stopped decreasing". With two or three levels, first-order convergence and a Monte Carlo noise floor, plateau and slow convergence cannot be told apart. A candidate that fails at the finest affordable level while still decreasing is never falsified.
- Corrections:
  - Falsify on any gate miss at the finest level within a predeclared cost budget.
  - Add a per-column ΣK ≤ d breach as a falsifier.
  - Predeclare the expected convergence order (O(h) for flat source).
- The built-in controls (16-image equality, linearity) hold by construction and are not evidence of physical accuracy.
- Criterion 3(b), BND_FAR/INT > 1.25, becomes over-strict when INT is near zero. Use an absolute floor.

**7. MAJOR (scope): the "no angle inside the medium" claim holds only for isotropic phase, homogeneous σ, index-matched boundaries.** (FACT / DERIVATION)
- With anisotropic phase functions (most real SSS, glass-like media), the scalar collision density is no longer sufficient. The method then becomes full angular transport with a different cost.
- Fresnel or total internal reflection adds reflected chords to W. That is tractable but outside the stated derivation.
- The generalisation argument is therefore a property of this test medium, not of an intrinsic K representation in general.
- It also leaves the brief's learning question unanswered. The candidate replaces learning with a solver.

**8. MINOR to MAJOR: outgoing-angle bias from flat Φout.** (DERIVATION, diffusive regime)
- With ψ(z) ∝ z + 0.71, flat-cell emission at h = 0.5 mm biases radiance by about −3.3 % at μ = 0.4 and −0.6 % at μ = 0.9.
- The outer 8×8 ring holds about 44 % of the projected solid angle.
- Near the source, where ψ is steeper, the bias is larger. This is HYPOTHESIS in magnitude. It feeds abs_fine16 and abs_native.

**9. MINOR: solver properties.**
- A two-grid or DSA overshoot is a passivity breach, not just an accuracy issue, so it must be counted against ΣK ≤ d.
- Inconsistent DSA degrades on cells about 0.5 mfp in size (the candidate flags this).
- Krylov methods lose exact linearity (the candidate flags this).
- FFT rounding makes symmetry equivariance hold to about 1e-7, not exactly.

**10. MINOR: cost margin.**
- Inside the GI solve, the red channel's internal convergence (about 260 plain Neumann sweeps) must be coupled to C. A preconditioner that ignores C may not accelerate the joint system.
- At 0.1–1 s per solve against a few seconds of re-rendering, the margin is unmeasured and depends on h (objections 1 and 5).
- The brief's 190 s exact oracle shows that a "physical oracle" is not automatically cheap.

## Strongest counterexample

Two outer-ring incoming directions on the same top port, at μ = 0.01 and μ = 0.1, with the same azimuth.

- **The cell inputs are nearly identical.** Both deposit essentially all first collisions in the top 0.5 mm cell layer, about 1 mfp downstream. Their lateral displacements differ by less than 1 %.
- **The true physics differs.** P_esc after first collision is 0.477 versus 0.380, so the multiply-scattered remainder is 0.523·q versus 0.620·q. That is a 19 % difference.
- **CP-K gives the same remainder for both:** 0.722·q at h = 0.5 mm. Both are wrong, and both overshoot passivity for red.
- **Refinement does not remove it.** Reducing h does not close this, because for any h one can choose μ ≪ h.
- **The size depends on bin integration.** After integrating over each 4×4 bin, the effect falls to a few percent of d (INFERENCE). It is largest at edges and rims, where two faces act, and it varies with the incident bin. That variation is exactly what the key metric con_coarse measures.

**Numbered objections**

1. **MAJOR: the partition-of-unity, passivity and reciprocity claims cannot all hold as built.** (DERIVATION)
   - The identity 1ᵀW + 1ᵀΦout = 1ᵀ only holds by construction if W and Φout are tallied from the same emission samples.
   - The candidate instead builds W by an analytic FFT convolution with analytic near-field cell integrals, and Φout from reverse-beam ray quadrature. Two separate quadratures leave a per-cell residual ε = 1ᵀW + 1ᵀΦout − 1ᵀ.
   - Redoing the brief's energy balance with this residual gives total output = 1ᵀq − (1−ω)1ᵀψ + ω εᵀψ.
   - Passivity therefore needs ω εᵀψ ≤ (1−ω)1ᵀψ. For red (ω = 0.999) that is a mean ε below about 1e-3 per cell, weighted by collision density. Near the boundary Φout is about 0.3–0.5, so it must be accurate to about 0.2–0.3 % everywhere.
   - Because 1ᵀψ is tens of collisions for red, any larger ε becomes a several-percent energy error and can break passivity.
   - Sampling W and Φout together would restore the partition but break exact symmetry between W[x,y] and W[y,x]; making that symmetric afterwards breaks the partition again.

   - Toy case: a grazing incoming bin, with first collisions within about 0.1 mm of the face. Take an outgoing bin with μ ≈ 0.2.
     - The flat-cell escape average over a 0.5 mm layer is 0.2(1 − e^{−2.5})/0.5 ≈ 0.37.
     - Collisions concentrated at z ≈ 0.1 give e^{−0.5} ≈ 0.61.
     - That is about a 1.7× error in the low-order escape for grazing-in and grazing-out combinations.
   - The error is worst for blue (ω = 0.9), where low orders dominate. It is also worst at edges and corners, where the source is thin against two faces at once.
   - A source integrated exactly by F_in still loses its depth position below the cell scale. Incoming-direction contrast (con_coarse) is largely depth and lateral-offset information, which is exactly what is lost.
   - Flat-source CP error is first order in h, so reaching about 1 % needs h of roughly 0.05–0.1 mm in a graded boundary layer. That rules out the FFT path (U).
   - Mitigation: also evaluate the double-scatter term (order 2) by fine quadrature, or use the linear-in-cell source from the start rather than as an option.

3. **MAJOR: the GI-solve cost is internally inconsistent.** (DERIVATION)

## 0. Three derivations that several responses depend on

**D1. Flight factorisation (DERIVATION).**
- With an escaping, index-matched boundary, the in-domain flight kernel is exactly `W_Ω(x,y) = vis_Ω(x,y)·W∞(|x−y|)`. A chord that leaves the medium ends that flight; re-entry belongs to C.
- Therefore `G_Ω = W_Ω + ω W_Ω G_Ω`.
- Visibility belongs inside every W factor, not on the resolvent.
- The exact split is `G_Ω = vis·W∞ + G_Ω^(≥2)`. The first term is exact, analytic and carries the singular near-diagonal part. The second term covers two or more flights and is smoother.
- Domain monotonicity also holds for the multi-flight remainder: `0 ≤ G_Ω^(≥2) ≤ G∞^(≥2)`.

**D2. Exact energy identity that needs only geometry (DERIVATION).**
- Let `P_esc(y) = 1 − Σ_z W_Ω(z,y)`. This is exact geometry, and it is exact on the lattice if P_esc is defined from the same discrete W.
- Let `R = (I − ωW)^-1 = I + ωG`. Then `1ᵀ(I − ωW) = uᵀ` with `u = (1−ω) + ωP_esc`, so `uᵀR = 1ᵀ`.
- Equivalently, for each column x: `Σ_y u(y)·Ĝ(y,x) = 1 − P_esc(x)`.
- If Ĝ ≥ 0 satisfies this with "≤", and Φout's total escape weight from each site equals P_esc, then the total output per source bin is ≤ ω·d ≤ d. Exact passivity follows with no solve.
- One apply of Ĝᵀ to u gives the column scalings.

**D3. Monotone nesting and exactly computable bases (DERIVATION).**
- A dent removes medium, so Ω_dent ⊂ Ω_rest. Hence `G_dent ≤ G_rest` pointwise for pairs in Ω_dent.
- The same holds for the chart families: `G_quarter-slab ≤ G_semi-slab ≤ G_slab ≤ G∞`.
- G_slab reduces to a 1-D Fredholm problem in depth for each in-plane wavenumber. G_semi-slab reduces to a 2-D cross-section problem for each wavenumber along the side. Both can be solved deterministically to high accuracy, with no learning.

**Adversarial review of C4 (NSM): collision-order-split port-space operator**

**Checks that hold**
- **Tail identity.** Summing ω^{n+1} λ^n over n > N gives ω^{N+2} λ^{N+1} / (1 − ωλ). So with all modes kept, NSM equals the underlying discrete operator. (DERIVATION)
- **Sign of the spectrum.** The continuum isotropic flight kernel e^{−r}/(4πr²) (r in mfp) has Fourier symbol arctan(k)/k > 0. Its restriction to a bounded body is positive semidefinite, so the eigenvalues lie in [0, 1). This holds discretely only for a Galerkin, exactly symmetric discretisation. (FACT / DERIVATION)
- **Mode count.** I used extrapolated slab thickness ≈ 11.4 mfp and width ≈ 101.4 mfp, so vertical k_z = mπ/11.4. The cut λ ≥ 0.84 corresponds to |k| ≤ 0.81 mm⁻¹, which keeps m = 1 and 2 only. Counting lateral (p, q) gives about 474 + 290 ≈ 760 modes, in line with the candidate's ~1e3. (DERIVATION under a separable diffusion-like approximation; ESTIMATE)
- **Source identity.** There is no hidden dependence on source identity. Every column comes from the geometry alone, so no source is extrapolated. (DERIVATION)

**Objections**

1. **[MAJOR] Not an answer to the stated problem; it is a compressed table of the solver.**
   - The brief asks for a representation that generalises to unsupervised sources "without memorising per-source responses".
   - NSM stores about 1.8e9 entries of per-source responses. They are computed rather than learned, and "physics validity is delegated to C1".
   - The candidate concedes C1 rebuilds in ≤ 1 s and applies in 5–40 ms. Against C1 alone, NSM's only gain is application speed, and it pays a 10–60 s dent rebuild and a 10–30 min first build for it.
   - Its first build is also 3–10× slower than the existing exact oracle the brief reports (190 s for a full update).
   - (INFERENCE)

2. **[MAJOR] Application is unlikely to be meaningfully faster than C1.**
   - Each near-field application must read about 3.5 GB of fp16 data. On a 12 GB-class GPU with roughly 0.4–0.9 TB/s memory bandwidth, that takes about 4–9 ms per RGB application before the tail.
   - The claimed 2–5 ms is therefore optimistic, and the result overlaps C1's lower bound of 5 ms. The candidate's own falsifier ("application not faster than C1") is at real risk.
   - Orbit and translation sharing add gather overhead and do not apply to dented poses.
   - (DERIVATION from the candidate's numbers; hardware bandwidth is an ESTIMATE)

3. **[MAJOR] Non-negativity is structurally violated and cannot be repaired without densifying.**
   - Cutting off the spectrum of a positive kernel produces signed, oscillating error. The truncation shell at k ≈ 0.81 ± 0.07 mm⁻¹ gives roughly J0-like lateral ringing. Its amplitude decays only algebraically, while the true red signal decays roughly exponentially, as about e^{−0.28 r/mm}.
   - Far receivers therefore get sign-wrong entries with arbitrarily large relative error. Integrated, the negative mass is plausibly only about 0.3 % of a column, but non-negativity is a CONTRACT property.
   - Clamping entries would densify the 245,760 × 61,440 operator. Clamping the output, max(Kx, 0), is nonlinear.
   - The passivity check is also too weak. A signed K can satisfy Σ K ≤ d per column while Σ |K| exceeds it. A Perron bound on the coupled spectral radius needs |K|, which cannot be computed cheaply from the low-rank form.
   - The candidate's 1 % negative-mass falsifier is a self-relaxed contract.
   - (DERIVATION for the sign structure; magnitude is an ESTIMATE / HYPOTHESIS)

4. **[MAJOR] The dent "influence region" is underspecified, and the natural reading misses sources whose transport changed although their geometry did not.**
   - A 6 mm dent in a 10 mm slab thins the body locally to 4 mm. Red diffusive transmission under the dent roughly doubles, (z0 + ze)/(L + 2ze) ≈ 0.15 → 0.31, and most of that change sits in orders ≤ 40.
   - Bottom-face sources beneath the dent (about 1e2–1e3 ports) and side or rim sources near an edge dent therefore need recomputation.
   - The influence radius should be the dent radius plus the window (15 mm) plus the low-order spread: about 35–45 mm, or roughly 800 top ports plus a comparable number on the bottom.
   - This is about 2× the candidate's 1e3 ports, which pushes the rebuild toward its own 60 s falsifier.
   - (DERIVATION / ESTIMATE)

5. **[MAJOR] The window is defined per face and fails at side faces, rims and corners.**
   - A side face is only 4 ports tall, so "12×12 ports plus the opposite face" is undefined there.
   - Near-edge and corner sources need the window to wrap across adjacent faces by 3-D distance. A per-face index window would drop exactly the boundary contexts on which every learned approach so far has failed (BND_FAR).
   - (INFERENCE)
   - The candidate's "coarse correction" for the window deficit is also unspecified. That deficit is a positive-definite bias, concentrated in a 15–25 mm annulus. Measured per coarse cell, the relative error there can be tens of percent even when the global error is 1–3 %. (ESTIMATE)

6. **[MAJOR] The "albedo-generic" novelty is hollow for most of the energy.**
   - Only the tail is albedo-generic. The near field is stored per channel (the 1.8e9 count already includes 3 channels).
   - The near field carries the directional contrast and about 98 % of blue energy (0.9^42 ≈ 0.012). It also carries most green energy.
   - Storing per-order near-field blocks to make it albedo-generic would cost about 40 × 3.5 GB. Even an 8–10-term Chebyshev expansion in ω would exceed 12 GB.
   - The claim also requires σ_t to be equal across channels and the phase function to be isotropic. Both hold here, but they narrow "material edit" to "albedo edit".
   - From memory: albedo as a scalar filter on a geometric eigenbasis is the classical c-eigenvalue / collision-number expansion of one-speed neutron transport (Davison; Bell & Glasstone; Case & Zweifel). "Exact low orders plus diffusion-like remainder" mirrors photon beam diffusion (Habel, Christensen & Jarosz 2013) and quantized diffusion (d'Eon & Irving 2011).
   - (INFERENCE on novelty)

7. **[MAJOR] Subspace tracking under a dent is likely mis-costed.**
   - The spectrum is dense near the cut: about 760 modes in [0.84, 0.976], a mean spacing of about 2e-4.
   - Plain subspace iteration converges at about (λ_{k+1}/λ_k)^iter ≈ (1 − 2e-4)^iter.

   - In fairness, the error from poorly converged Ritz pairs near the cut is weighted by only about 5e-3. So this is a cost issue more than an accuracy issue.

   - (HYPOTHESIS; C1 is not visible to me)

**5. MAJOR: the 2–5 ms application estimate does not account for memory bandwidth.**
- Inside a GI solve, the incident field from C is dense across all ports and bins, so every iteration must stream the full near field (3.5–7 GB).
- On a 12 GB-class GPU (~400–700 GB/s) that is about 5–18 ms per RGB application, plus 2–3 ms for the tail. Symmetry-orbit storage adds irregular gathers on top.
- The candidate's criterion "application faster than C1 (5–40 ms)" is therefore not established. It may tie C1.
- 64-light batching amortises the bandwidth, but it does not help the 1-light iterative GI case.
- Label: ESTIMATE.

**6. MAJOR: the tail error bound is in the wrong norm.**
- The bound ω^{N+2}λ_cut^{N+1}/(1−ωλ_cut) ≈ 5e-3 holds in the M-weighted L2 norm.
- Passivity and the abs metrics are per-column flux (ℓ1) quantities. The spectral projector onto the slow modes is not contractive in ℓ1, so a localised boundary source projected onto ~1e3 smooth modes rings (Gibbs).
- The signed residual therefore spreads over the whole face. At far receivers it can be comparable to the true red response, which is small there.
- The proposed "column scale" passivity fix cannot remove negative entries. Clamping entries would keep the operator linear, but it requires densifying the low-rank tail and so gives up its storage advantage.
- The "< 1 % negative mass" criterion is too lenient to catch errors at far receivers.
- Label: DERIVATION for the norm mismatch; HYPOTHESIS for the magnitude.

**7. MAJOR: red is mostly tail, so the tail is not a small correction.**
- With a slab 10 mfp thick, the fundamental eigenvalue is λ₀ ≈ arctan(0.275)/0.275 ≈ 0.976 (extrapolated thickness ≈ 11.4 mfp).
- For red, ωλ₀ ≈ 0.975, which gives about 40 mean collisions in that mode. A random-walk exit-time estimate for a mid-plane source gives about 49 collisions.
- So a large share of red output from interior sources, plausibly 30–50 %, comes after order 41.
- The ~1e3-mode truncation and its ringing (objection 6) therefore control red accuracy. Red is also the channel where the brief records 5.5 % of energy outside the 128 nearest ports.
- Blue (ω^{41} ≈ 0.013) does not need the tail at all.
- Label: DERIVATION and ESTIMATE.

**8. MINOR: tracking the eigenmodes is ill-posed, though the damage is bounded.**
- Near λ_cut the spectrum is dense, with spacing around 1e-4. A 6 mm dent in a 10 mm slab is a large perturbation, so individual eigenvectors cannot be tracked and Davis–Kahan subspace stability fails without a gap.
- Error from modes assigned to the wrong side of the cut is bounded by f(λ_cut) ≈ 5e-3.
- Exact equivariance under the "keep whole eigenspaces" rule needs a symmetry-adapted (irrep-blocked) solve. Truncating at a tolerance mixes near-degenerate modes.
- "2–3e3 Lanczos matvecs" is optimistic for ~1e3 clustered eigenpairs with no gap. A Chebyshev filter or shift-invert would be needed.
- Label: INFERENCE.

**9. MINOR: the self-adjointness and symmetry assumptions depend on discretisation details.**
- M·W symmetric requires the cell-to-cell kernel to be collision-probability reciprocal (σ_x V_x P_{x→y} = σ_y V_y P_{y→x}), including the singular self-cell term.
- A Monte Carlo or approximately quadratured W breaks that symmetry, and Lanczos then gives non-real modes.
- Separately, multiplexed near-field solves leak window cross-talk at window edges (see objection 2).
- Label: DERIVATION.

**10. MAJOR (novelty): the claimed contribution is largely covered by prior work.**

- "Spectral projectors commute with the symmetry group" is a standard representation-theory fact.
- What remains plausibly new is a narrow combination: a collision-order split used as a stored port-space intrinsic K for a slab, with a symmetry-preserving truncation rule.
- Label: INFERENCE. Literature identities are verified for the first two rows only; the rest are from memory.

**11. MINOR: the falsification test cannot reach the brief's gates.**
- The test compares NSM only with C1 and "needs no MC." But the gates are defined against Monte Carlo references, so "≤ 1/3 of each gate margin relative to C1" is meaningful only once C1's own margin to MC is known on the INT/BND populations.
- Run before C1 is validated, the test is uninformative.
- Label: DERIVATION.

## Strongest counterexample

A single 25 mm-radius dent forces about 2.6e4 near-field columns to be recomputed (about 800 affected top ports plus 800 bottom ports, × 16 bins). Those sources are clustered within an 80 mm disc, so only about 2–4 can be multiplexed per batch without window cross-talk.

That means roughly 3e5–5e5 W applications. At the candidate's own 1–3 ms per application this is about 5–25 minutes per dent. That is beyond its own 60 s threshold, against C1 at ≤ 1 s and re-rendering at a few seconds.

The dented, non-convex medium also invalidates the FFT-speed W application that the estimate assumes. NSM therefore loses under exactly the geometry change the project targets, and wins only for static-geometry relighting that classical PRT already serves.

## Recommendation

**REJECT** as a candidate answer to the central question. It provides no generalisation mechanism and no learnability claim, and it is dominated under deformation.

It could be kept only as an optional, conditional runtime compression of C1 for static-geometry, lighting-heavy workloads, if C1 is independently validated against MC. Even then, it must first show bandwidth-measured application faster than C1 and red-channel ℓ1 tail errors and negative mass below MC noise.
