# Track A decision log (RETAIN / REVISE / REMOVE / UNRESOLVED)

> WL80A sealed record. Generated by the Workflow run named below; agent texts are reproduced verbatim (the Coordinator added only headings). Design-only thought experiment: nothing was implemented, trained or measured.

**Central mechanism sound?** NO. BGC is not sound as a source-generalisation mechanism. It is sound only as a passive parametrisation of K, and that part is repairable. The architecture is therefore REJECTED, not reconstructed.

**1. The distinguishing hypothesis has no identification mechanism (DERIVATION; failure pattern FACT).**
- BGC's distinguishing hypothesis is that pair-conditioned angular-spatial factors, generated from first-flight and relational encoders, produce held-out source transfer.
- At R >= 16, any nonnegative 16x64 block has nonnegative rank <= 16, so A3+A4 span the whole positive passive cone. ρ absorbs any column sum, and w and u are identifiable only as the product w·u.
- A5 adds information only inside the rest D4h orbit of a training source.
- The orbit-consistent bump construction w' = w(1 + λφψ) keeps every hard property: linear, positive, passive, exactly D4h-equivariant and identity-free. It reproduces every S16-R label and changes held-out boundary near-field contrast by O(1). That is exactly where WL72, WL75 and WL78 failed.

**2. The one new element targets the factor the record ranks as non-binding (FACT, re-checked this session).**
- WL75 line 195: on BND4_FAR, con_coarse is 0.159 for the frozen-global-basis G2 oracle against 0.803 / 0.824 for learned A75.
- WL75 line 308: "Global angular rank is not the bottleneck ... the spatial geometry -> T mapping is."
- A3 makes the angular factors outputs of that same learned geometry->coefficient map (INFERENCE: strictly less identified than ATO).

**3. The rest of BGC re-assembles mechanisms already built (FACT, re-checked).**
- WL76 FIA (lines 102-110, 207-208) already had a first-collision anchor rescaled to 1−β, an escape mass ρ, a tail share τ, a softmax over the 512-NN ports plus a 240-cell tail, and invariant features. It ended INSUFFICIENT FIT.
- WL78 W+UV-Q relational inputs gave an equivalent-context difference Q78/A75 of 1.00 / 1.03 (line 246) and a seed-dependent BND_FAR of 0.668 / 0.860 (line 275).
- WL79/80 already built the rest D4h maps.

**4. The A4 normalisation is structurally wrong (DERIVATION).**
- An independently predicted ρ duplicates Σ K.
- It produces a receiver-uniform spurious factor (ρ'/ρ)(S/S') under edits outside the pair contexts.
- It makes exact reciprocity impossible unless ρ ∝ S/(m·d).
- The A4 repairs below fix validity but add no identification.

**5. Repairs that would add identification change the central hypothesis (DERIVATION).** Two such repairs exist:
- (a) compute the source-dependent first-collision part exactly and learn only the source-independent post-collision Q (WL76 E0), which is the FIA family;
- (b) build the operator as the reciprocity-symmetric bilinear form m_i K_ij ∝ m_i d_i m_j d_j <p1_i, 𝒢 p1_j>, which is a learned symmetric Green's kernel.

Both are different architectures. They are reported as such and not adopted.

**Caveat.** This verdict rests on derivation plus the recorded history, not on a measurement of a BGC instance. Whether some generator's inductive bias happens to transfer is empirically unmeasured. As a mechanism claim it is unsupported, and the stated constraints provably do not deliver it.

### A1 Incident physics encoder (collision probability, first-collision mean and covariance) — **REVISE**
- **Defect:** Four defects. (i) Collision probability equals d = 1−β, so it is redundant (FACT, WL76 E[c] = 1−β). (ii) Mean and covariance are not sufficient statistics: E0 needs the whole first-collision measure p1. The second-order closure of single scatter (25-36 % of collided energy, WL76 post hoc diagnostic, FACT) has depth-factor errors of +10 % at μ_i = μ_o, +22 % at 2x and −18 % at 4x; a cumulant closure is worse (DERIVATION). Edge-row chord mixtures match (d, μ, Σ) but differ in κ3 within the ~1 mm near field (DERIVATION; magnitude HYPOTHESIS). (iii) The encoder is source-only, but the exact form (E1) is symmetric in the source density and the reversed-receiver density (DERIVATION). (iv) How the features are computed is unspecified. Monte Carlo quadrature repeats WL76's 75 s rebuild (FACT), q-noise floors (WL77A, non-evidence) and β resampling differences up to 0.075 (WL74A/WL68, FACT).
- **Argument / counterexample:** E0 (FACT, WL76 Stage 1, 36/36 packets): K = d·E_{x~p1}[Q(x→j,o)]. A Taylor closure of Q about the mean is exact only if Q is at most quadratic over the support of p1. Near a boundary Q varies on the 1 mm mfp scale, which is also the spread of p1, so the truncation is O(1). Counterexample: a face-on semi-infinite single scatter has an exact depth factor μ_o/(μ_i+μ_o), and the second-order closure misses it by the amounts above (DERIVATION). Moments do appear adequate at coarse resolution: the WL74A span of x, y, r² holds 94.5-98.8 % of the centred incoming dependence (FACT).
- **Modification:** Five changes. (1) Drop collision probability as a feature. (2) Compute d(i,a) = 1 − β(i,a) deterministically: β = (1/m(i,a)) ∫_port ∫_bin exp(−σ_t·t(x,ω)) cosθ dω dA on a fixed stratified port × bin grid of analytic chords, with no Monte Carlo. (3) Keep (μ, Σ), if at all, only as auxiliary descriptors explicitly labelled non-sufficient, and compute them analytically per chord. (4) If first-flight statistics are used, apply the same statistic to the receiver in the reversed direction, so the input respects E1's symmetry. (5) Using exact p1 as a quadrature measure is the FIA mechanism. That is a change of architecture and is not adopted here.
- **Why it addresses the defect:** A deterministic d removes the quadrature-noise and resampling confound from the budget, which is also A4's passivity bound. Dropping the redundant feature removes a duplicated input. Adding the receiver-side statistic removes the source/receiver asymmetry that E1 forbids. Labelling the moments as non-sufficient stops them being cited as physical sufficient statistics. The modification does not make A1 sufficient.
- **New assumptions:** Homogeneous, isotropic, index-matched medium with exact chord geometry from the current mesh. σ_t's behaviour under deformation must be declared: world-space constant or mass-conserving. The proposal leaves this unspecified (D8, INFERENCE).
- **New cost:** About 3840 x 16 cells x ~256 chords ≈ 1.6e7 ray casts per rebuild (DERIVATION). Order of milliseconds on GPU (INFERENCE), against 74.3 s of Monte Carlo quadrature in WL76 (FACT). The receiver-side statistics double that cost.
- **New risks:** A learned decoder may still treat the moments as a code for the rest-geometry chord family. It would then mis-decode tilted, curved or dented ports (INFERENCE). The analytic chord grid has its own discretisation error, so its convergence in grid size must be declared.
- **Expected effect on source generalisation:** Small. A1 encodes only the source-side p1, which is the part that already worked: WL76 per-incoming-bin energy profile relative L2 0.023 (FACT). Transfer failed in the post-collision geometry→coefficient map (WL68, WL75, FACT), and A1 does not touch that map. Expected effect: removal of noise-driven spurious contrast, with no gain on out-of-orbit boundary transfer (INFERENCE).
- **Falsifying experiment:** Non-learned analytic check, not authorised and not recommended here. On rest and dented geometry, compare the deterministic exact-quadrature single-scatter response at edge-row and grazing ports with the response reconstructed from (d, μ, Σ) through the best possible decoder fitted on interior ports. If the relative near-field error at edge ports is at or below the reference noise floor, the insufficiency claim is falsified for this fixture.

### A2 Boundary relation encoder (relative displacement, frames, boundary distances, thickness, pairwise context) — **REVISE**
- **Defect:** Three defects. (i) Endpoint context plus displacement is insufficient when geometry changes between source and receiver. Example: a 4 mm underside dent midway along a 12 mm pair leaves all endpoint inputs unchanged but changes red transfer by a factor of about e^{-1.2} (DERIVATION, diffusion approximation; magnitude HYPOTHESIS). (ii) Several routes leak identity: index-ordered k-NN ties, 1,114-5,852 edges per operation (FACT, WL80); unclipped distances to the six faces, which are an affine bijection of position up to D4h (DERIVATION); the face-dependent t/b frame convention (FACT, WL74A); first-triangle frames on dents (FACT, WL79). (iii) The clauses 'must not discard absolute information' and 'no identity key' contradict each other in a finite box unless a range is specified (DERIVATION). Also, for this homogeneous medium K depends only on current geometry, ports and frame convention, so rest or material coordinates add no physical information (DERIVATION).
- **Argument / counterexample:** Screening estimate (DERIVATION, diffusion lowest slab mode): 1/e lateral decay ≈ 3.5 / 3.1 / 1.6 mm for R/G/B, set mostly by thickness (H_eff ≈ 11.3 mm). Physical dependence on geometry beyond about 5 decay lengths is negligible, and dependence inside the corridor is O(1). WL78 already removed absolute keys. The interior was fixed, but frame-related equivalent contexts were not: Q78/A75 1.00 / 1.03 (FACT, WL78 line 246). Removing leakage is therefore necessary but has been shown not to be sufficient.
- **Modification:** Six changes. (1) Clip every boundary-distance input at a declared channel-dependent radius r_c ≈ 5/κ_c (about 18 / 16 / 8 mm), justified by the screening derivation. (2) Replace endpoint-only context with a corridor descriptor: geometry sampled over a band of width about sqrt(r·ℓ) around the i-j segment, out to r_c. (3) Use radius-defined neighbour sets with every tie included (no index order). (4) Use one frame convention defined from geometry, not from triangle index, and state where it is not unique (D4-symmetric lattice points). (5) Forbid rest or material coordinates as inputs. (6) State the σ_t density law under deformation.
- **Why it addresses the defect:** Clipping makes the distance inputs a local context rather than a position fingerprint, so leakage route (ii) closes. The corridor descriptor restores the physically relevant intervening geometry (i). Tie-free sets and a geometric frame remove two documented leakage routes. Forbidding rest coordinates removes a channel that, by the derivation, carries only identity.
- **New assumptions:** The diffusion screening radius is adequate near edges and corners, where the approximation is crude within about 1 mfp. A geometric frame can be defined consistently on deformed ports, which WL79 showed is non-trivial.
- **New cost:** Corridor sampling per pair: about 3x10^5 pairs within 12 mm (DERIVATION) times corridor samples. Order 10^7 geometry queries per rebuild (INFERENCE). Rebuild order tens of ms (unmeasured).
- **New risks:** Clipping at r_c may remove information about a second edge, as in TBE-far (INFERENCE). The geometric frame may be undefined at symmetric points, which needs a tie-break that could reintroduce identity. Corridor descriptors increase input dimension and therefore memorisation capacity (INFERENCE).
- **Expected effect on source generalisation:** Necessary conditions only. Removing leakage helped the interior in WL78 but not boundary or out-of-orbit contexts (FACT). No source of identification is added for out-of-orbit boundary contexts, because the bump construction is unaffected (DERIVATION). Expected: deformation behaviour more physical (corridor), with boundary transfer unchanged (INFERENCE).
- **Falsifying experiment:** Not authorised and not recommended. On two physically identical contexts that differ only in absolute position, ties or triangulation, every encoder input must be bitwise equal; any difference falsifies leak-freedom. The corridor sufficiency claim is falsified if the reference K for the midway-dent pair matches the undeformed K within noise.

### A3 Conditional angular-spatial operator (pair-conditioned u, v, w from a shared network) — **REMOVE**
- **Defect:** Five defects. (i) It is BGC's only new element relative to WL57-80, and it targets the factor recorded as non-binding: WL75 BND4_FAR G2 oracle 0.159 against learned 0.803 / 0.824, and 'global angular rank is not the bottleneck' (FACT, re-checked at WL75 lines 195 and 308). (ii) It removes the one component that transferred trivially, the shared A/O prior and denoiser, and makes the angular factors outputs of the geometry→coefficient map that failed (WL68: correlation ≈ 0 at unseen sources, FACT). (iii) At R ≥ 16 it constrains nothing; per pair it is barely compressive (R·81 = 648 degrees of freedom vs 1,024 entries at R = 8). (iv) Storage of 243R floats per pair exceeds the dense 3,072-float block at R ≥ 13. Apply costs about 81x a global ATO per pair because a pair-dependent v cannot be factored out of the source sum (DERIVATION). (v) Dichotomy: a nonnegative linear decoder over fixed atoms is exactly a higher-rank global ATO, while a nonlinear decoder carries the cost in (iv) (DERIVATION).
- **Argument / counterexample:** Bound (DERIVATION from FACT): a perfect angular family can remove at most the oracle residual, about 0.16 of the ~0.80 BND4_FAR error. At least about 0.64 lies in the learned spatial map, which A3 enlarges. The WL68 and WL75 pattern 'fits better, transfers no better' (FACT) is the expected outcome of more pair capacity. The recurrent late-training drift (@2000 beats final in WL72, 73 and 75; Q78 0.63→0.86, FACT) gains room as capacity grows (INFERENCE). In fairness, pair-conditioned rank is physically motivated, since far diffusive pairs tend to rank 1 (DERIVATION), and it removes FIA's ring-lobe fit floor (0.45, FACT WL76 line 270) and the rest orientation non-closure. Both are fit properties, not identification.
- **Modification:** Remove pair-conditioned free angular factors as BGC's transfer mechanism. Each alternative is a different architecture and is not adopted: (a) return to a shared global basis, which is ATO; (b) shared atoms with pair-dependent nonnegative coefficients, which is a higher-rank ATO; (c) relative-azimuth harmonics on a source-independent post-collision response, which is the WL77 FIA-AZ family.
- **Why it addresses the defect:** It removes the element whose rationale contradicts the recorded attribution, and whose capacity enlarges the unidentified hypothesis space without adding constraints.
- **New assumptions:** None added. Removal returns to assumptions already tested in WL68-80.
- **New cost:** None. Avoids the 3.8 GB (N128 R8) to 30.6 GB (N512 R16) fp32 factor caches and the ~81x apply cost (DERIVATION).
- **New risks:** If the per-pair nonnegative rank at target accuracy is ≤ 4 for near and edge pairs (unmeasured, HYPOTHESIS), removal discards a representation that is compact and well motivated for fit. No worklog has measured per-pair nonnegative rank; the WL77A rank ~4 is an SVD point statistic and non-evidence.
- **Expected effect on source generalisation:** Removal neither improves nor worsens transfer relative to ATO-family baselines. Keeping A3 is expected to worsen held-out angular error through variance on low-SNR far-receiver 64-bin labels (INFERENCE) and to leave boundary transfer unidentified (DERIVATION).
- **Falsifying experiment:** Not authorised and not recommended. A matched oracle comparison on archived references: per-source free pair-conditioned rank-R factors vs per-source free T with frozen global rank-16 A/O, both fitted on the same sources and scored on BND4_FAR. If the pair-conditioned oracle beats the 0.159 G2 oracle substantially and a learned version closes more than half of the 0.80→0.159 gap at matched supervision, the 'non-binding factor' objection is falsified.

### A4 Energy normalisation: bound, budget, measure, domain, local/far partition — **REVISE**
- **Defect:** Five defects. (i) The bound d is too loose: a first collision absorbs with probability 1−ω, so Σ K ≤ ω_c·d (DERIVATION; WL76's Q already used Σ Q ≤ albedo, FACT). (ii) Port-area × projected-solid-angle weights are missing from ΣZ, which is wrong under deformation (INFERENCE). (iii) The normalisation domain is unrestricted: strictly positive activations leak to unreachable receivers and cross optical domains, against FNTO K/C ownership (INFERENCE). (iv) There is no single budget across the local and long-range parts: separate normalisation reaches 2dρ, and local-only normalisation pushes the missing far energy, 5.5 / 3.2 / 0.1 % for interior sources outside 128-NN (FACT, WL78 line 119, re-checked), into the near field (DERIVATION). (v) A Monte Carlo d makes the bound relative to a noisy budget, off by up to 0.075 absolute (FACT, WL74A/WL68).
- **Argument / counterexample:** Per-input normalisation bounds the ℓ1-induced norm (maximum column sum in power units). With a passive C this gives ||KC||_1 < 1 and a convergent coupled solve, which directly answers the WL41/42 and WL50 divergences (FACT, AGENTS.md). The direction of A4 is therefore correct; its bound, measure, domain and partition are wrong or unspecified (DERIVATION).
- **Modification:** K(i,a→j,o,c) = d(i,a)·ω_c·χ_D(i,j)·m(j,o)·z(i,a,j,o,c) / max(1, Σ_{j',o'} χ_D(i,j')·m(j',o')·z(i,a,j',o',c)). Here d is the deterministic budget (A1), χ_D is the exact same-optical-domain reachability mask, m is port area × projected solid angle on the current mesh, and z ≥ 0 is a density. z = z_loc on N(i) and z_far on the complement, with disjoint supports and one shared denominator. The ρ and ε terms are removed (see the normalisation entry).
- **Why it addresses the defect:** The bound becomes physical (≤ ω d). The measure makes Σ a power sum under deformation. χ_D gives exact zeros outside the domain and preserves FNTO ownership. One shared denominator over disjoint supports makes passivity hold for the whole K and prevents energy being double-counted or pushed into the near field. A deterministic d removes the noisy-budget confound.
- **New assumptions:** The reachability mask is computable exactly from geometry. A single scalar ω_c per channel (homogeneous medium). The local/far supports form an exact partition of receivers, with ties included.
- **New cost:** One extra area-weighted sum per column, which is negligible. Computing χ_D needs domain connectivity, already available in FNTO.
- **New risks:** The max(1,·) projection has the form of an energy clamp, which earlier briefs excluded as post hoc stabilisation. It must be declared as part of the operator contract before any result, never added after a failure. Gradients are non-smooth at Σ = 1. Where the bound binds, the receiver-coupling artefact returns (see the normalisation entry).
- **Expected effect on source generalisation:** Validity only. Passivity and the coupled-solve convergence guarantee are preserved, and red near-field mass bias from a missing far budget (~+5.5 %, DERIVATION from FACT) is avoided. No effect on identification of out-of-orbit contexts (DERIVATION).
- **Falsifying experiment:** Not authorised and not recommended. Analytic check on archived reference K: compute Σ_{j,o} m·K/(d·ω) per column. Any column above 1 beyond the reference noise falsifies the ω·d bound for this medium. Also check whether nonzero reference transport crosses χ_D = 0 pairs.

### A5 Angular frame transformation (finite-group D4h averaging) — **REVISE**
- **Defect:** Six defects. (i) Exact only for the rest D4h group; at dented poses the bin residual is 0.06-0.16 with non-bijective outgoing maps (FACT, WL79). (ii) Continuous in-plane SO(2) is not a bin permutation of the 8x8 concentric grid, and a per-port frame-gauge change (e.g. 45° after a re-mesh) cannot be made exact (DERIVATION). (iii) It adds no information outside the orbit of a training source (DERIVATION; bump construction). (iv) Inference-time averaging costs 16 generator evaluations. The averaged block is a sum of 16 permuted rank-R terms, generically full rank, which breaks the factor cache (DERIVATION). (v) Averaging with a noisy d can push an averaged column above its own budget: β noise 0.58-0.60 % relative (FACT, WL79).
- **Argument / counterexample:** With a permutation action, the group average is an orthogonal projection. Against an equivariant truth it cannot increase L2 error, and it preserves positivity and row normalisation (DERIVATION). That part is sound. Its scope is the rest orbit, which WL79/80 already handle by exact transformed labels (FACT). The orbit-balanced basis cut the orientation gap 1.41→1.15 (WL80, FACT), and WL80 notes this gap is much smaller than the remaining learned boundary-transfer error (FACT).
- **Modification:** Four changes. (1) Restrict the claim explicitly to rest D4h with no deformation and no continuous-rotation claim. (2) Use the exact maps for training-time label augmentation and as a predeclared equivariance test, not as inference-time averaging. (3) If inference averaging is used, average the entire assembled K, including d and the denominator, so the per-column budget is preserved, and store the result as a dense local block. (4) Report the per-port frame gauge as an unresolved limitation.
- **Why it addresses the defect:** It removes the 16x inference cost and the rank-breaking of the cache. It keeps the exact rest symmetry where it is valid. Averaging after assembly preserves passivity under a noisy d. The scope statement prevents an unsupported deformation or continuous-symmetry claim.
- **New assumptions:** Rest-geometry exact maps as built and verified in WL79 Stage 0.
- **New cost:** Training-time augmentation increases data presented by up to 16x but adds no new labels. If inference averaging is kept: 16x generator cost and dense storage (6.04 GB fp32 at N128, DERIVATION).
- **New risks:** Augmentation can over-weight the orbit images of the 4 boundary sources and specialise further to their orbits (INFERENCE; cf. the WL80 2-18 % original-orientation cost, FACT).
- **Expected effect on source generalisation:** Removes the at-rest orientation non-closure for in-orbit held-out sources. No effect on out-of-orbit boundary contexts or deformed poses (DERIVATION). Bounded by the 1.15-1.41x orientation-gap scale (FACT), which is small compared with the learned transfer gap.
- **Falsifying experiment:** Not authorised and not recommended. Evaluate the assembled K (averaged or augmented-trained) on the exact transformed images of held-out in-orbit sources. A transformed/original error ratio above the replica noise falsifies exact rest equivariance. A dented-pose test is expected to fail by construction, since the maps are undefined there.

### A6 Computational representation (local sparse pairs + separate long-range part; cached pair latents) — **UNRESOLVED**
- **Defect:** Six defects. (i) Underspecified: the support rule, how the far part is represented, and whether latents are encoded or fitted per geometry are all open. Fitted latents are a learned per-pair identity key (DERIVATION). (ii) The local/far energy partition is fragile. At N128 the red far share is about 6-22 % (diffusion estimate, DERIVATION) and 5.5 % for interior sources (FACT, WL78), so the far part must be accurate. N512 R16 caches are 15-31 GB and do not fit 12 or 16 GB GPUs (DERIVATION). (iii) A hard k-NN cutoff with index-ordered ties changes membership discontinuously under deformation (INFERENCE; ties FACT, WL80). (iv) Decoding only for active illumination saves little: the denominator needs every j', C needs every exiting port, and a sun prunes at most the u share, ≤ 20 % (DERIVATION). (v) A global low-rank far term would need rank of about (100/3.5)² ≈ 800 (INFERENCE). (vi) The normalisation and pairwise context widen invalidation to the edit region dilated by the support radius, and the geometry/B rebuild already dominates selective updates: 0.32 of 0.33 s (FACT, WL59).
- **Argument / counterexample:** The cost depends on the per-pair nonnegative rank at target accuracy, which no worklog has measured (FACT: all existing rank evidence is for global bases). If R ≤ 4 suffices, N128 needs 1.9 GB fp32. If R ≥ 13, the factors exceed the dense block and BGC is a neural generator of a dense local K (DERIVATION). With A3 removed, the pair-factor cache has no purpose in BGC.
- **Modification:** No architecture modification is adopted. Any successor would need to state: (1) a radius-defined, tie-inclusive support with a smooth partition-of-unity window across the local/far boundary; (2) a far part represented as a separable hierarchical-cell term u_far(i,a)·G_cell·v_far(j,o) inside A4's single shared denominator; (3) encoder-only latents; (4) a fused receiver-major apply kernel, never a naive einsum (~24 GB of intermediates, DERIVATION); (5) a memory and time budget declared against A75/ATO on the 12 GB target before any result.
- **Why it addresses the defect:** It closes the latent-identity route, the double-counting and passivity gap, and the membership discontinuity, and makes the cost claim checkable. It does not establish feasibility, so the label stays UNRESOLVED.
- **New assumptions:** Past about 8 mfp, transport is separable (angular memory lost; INFERENCE). Cell-level far coupling is accurate enough for this slab's far share.
- **New cost:** Order-of-magnitude derivations: N128 R8 factor cache 3.82 GB fp32. One-light apply ≥ ~4 ms memory-bound, 64-light apply ≥ ~5-10 ms with an ideal fused kernel; realistic 0.1-1 s in this codebase's style (INFERENCE from WL76 FIA 0.25-0.35 s CSR, FACT). Latent decode 15-30 ms per K application at N128 R8, repeated over 6-20 applications per coupled solve unless cached (INFERENCE).
- **New risks:** The smooth window introduces a second length scale to declare. The far cells may carry identity if indexed by absolute cell id (HYPOTHESIS). Selective-recompute savings are bounded by the dilated invalidation set (INFERENCE). Any cost comparison is subject to the mandatory baseline gate in docs/BASELINE_ROLES_AND_EVIDENCE_STRATEGY.md (Neural Full Recompute ≠ Cycles).
- **Expected effect on source generalisation:** None on identification. A6 is a cost and partition element. An inaccurate far part would bias the red near field through the shared denominator (DERIVATION).
- **Falsifying experiment:** Not authorised and not recommended. A non-learned analysis on archived reference K: per-pair nonnegative matrix factorisation rank at the target relative error, split into near (< 5 mm), edge/corner and far pairs, with the reference-noise floor estimated from replicas. Per-pair rank ≥ 13 at target accuracy for near or edge pairs falsifies any compressive pair-factor cache.

### Operator form: Z factorisation Σ_r w(i,j,r,c) u(i,j,r,a,c) v(i,j,r,o,c) — **REVISE**
- **Defect:** Three defects. (i) w and u are both pair-dependent, so only w·u is identifiable. 'Spatial weight' and 'incoming angular response' are a gauge choice (w→λw, u→u/λ), not a structural prior (DERIVATION). (ii) A uniform per-(i,a) rescaling of Z cancels in the normalisation, so any regulariser on Z has no fixed scale (DERIVATION). (iii) The u/v roles are asymmetric, while the exact operator is a symmetric bilinear form in source and reversed-receiver first-collision densities (E1, DERIVATION). The factorisation therefore cannot express reciprocity structurally.
- **Argument / counterexample:** E1 (DERIVATION, specialist, consistent with the WL77A record): m(i,a)K(i,a→j,o) = (ω/4πσ_t)·[m d]_i,a·[m d]_j,ō·<p1(·|i,a), 𝒢 p1(·|j,ō)>. Any per-pair nonnegative block of nonnegative rank ≤ R is representable, so the form is valid but vacuous at R ≥ 16.
- **Modification:** For the record only (BGC rejected): merge w into u (u ≥ 0 unnormalised, v ≥ 0 normalised per r over o with the measure m), which removes the gauge. With A4's max(1,·) projection, Z carries absolute scale, so the scale gauge in (ii) disappears. Making the factorisation symmetric (u and v generated by one shared map applied to source and reversed receiver) changes the hypothesis toward the symmetric Green's-kernel architecture and is not adopted.
- **Why it addresses the defect:** Removing the gauges makes the factors identifiable up to permutation over r. An absolutely scaled Z gives regularisers a fixed scale.
- **New assumptions:** v is normalised under the current-mesh measure m(j,o).
- **New cost:** None beyond the original (243R → 240R floats per pair).
- **New risks:** Absolute scale must now be learned per entry without a normalisation prior. Where the network over-predicts, the projection binds and receiver coupling returns (INFERENCE).
- **Expected effect on source generalisation:** None on transfer. Identifiability of the parametrisation only (DERIVATION).
- **Falsifying experiment:** Not authorised and not recommended. On a fixed fitted instance, verify numerically that rescaling w by λ and u by 1/λ leaves K unchanged (gauge exists), and that after merging no continuous gauge remains, by checking the rank of the Jacobian of K with respect to the factors.

### Operator form: normalisation d·ρ·Z/(ε + ΣZ) (independent ρ, ε) — **REVISE**
- **Defect:** Four defects. (i) ρ duplicates Σ_{j,o} K, the physical total exit, which is not an independent quantity (DERIVATION). (ii) With a source-local ρ, any edit outside the pair contexts rescales every near receiver and bin by the common factor (ρ'/ρ)(S/S'). The physical change is δK_ij ∝ ψ_i(x_E)ψ†_j(x_E), which is receiver-dependent and close to 0 off the corridor; a hypothetical example gives about −5 % uniformly (DERIVATION; magnitude SPECULATION). (iii) Exact reciprocity requires ρ_i = c·S_i/(m_i d_i), so an independently predicted ρ is generically non-reciprocal, while the oracle K is reciprocal to 0.998-1.000 (FACT, AGENTS.md WL53 summary). (iv) Through ε the realised column sum is dρ·ΣZ/(ε+ΣZ) < dρ, so ρ is not the realised fraction, and ρ trades off against |Z| (DERIVATION). Common-denominator coupling also enlarges the K-side transport-dirty set.
- **Argument / counterexample:** The edit counterexample: with near receivers at 0.60 and the tail 0.30→0.35, K_near = 0.9·0.60/0.95 = 0.568, about −5 % uniformly, where physics gives about 0 (hypothetical numbers, SPECULATION; structure DERIVATION). Any fix that removes the artefact forces ρ ∝ S, the same condition reciprocity requires, so ρ cannot be free.
- **Modification:** Remove ρ and ε. Use A4's projection K = dω·m·z / max(1, Σ m z). While Σ m z ≤ 1 the operator is entrywise local: K_ij depends only on z_ij, so there is no cross-receiver coupling and no ρ. Reciprocity cannot be built into any per-column normalisation. It can be imposed only as a declared training loss, or structurally through the symmetric bilinear form, which is a different architecture and not adopted.
- **Why it addresses the defect:** Removes the redundant quantity and its inconsistency with Σ K. Eliminates the receiver-uniform edit artefact wherever the bound does not bind. Removes the ε entanglement. Below the bound, reciprocity is no longer obstructed by the normalisation, because a reciprocal z yields a reciprocal K.
- **New assumptions:** The learned z stays below the budget on most columns (Σ m z ≤ 1). The physical exit fraction after first collision is strictly below ω for this index-matched slab.
- **New cost:** Negligible.
- **New risks:** Where the projection binds, the coupling artefact and non-reciprocity return. The binding fraction must be reported. The max(1,·) form must be predeclared (see A4 risk). Without normalisation as a learning prior, early training may be less stable (INFERENCE).
- **Expected effect on source generalisation:** Removes a spurious receiver-uniform edit response and keeps reciprocity available as a constraint. Reciprocity could turn the 16 supervised source columns into 16 rows at 16-bin resolution (DERIVATION; bin nesting INFERENCE), but mainly in the far field. No identification of out-of-orbit near-field boundary contexts follows (DERIVATION).
- **Falsifying experiment:** Not authorised and not recommended. Analytic check on archived reference K: for an edit outside a pair's context (e.g. WL72 te2 ports 57-75 mm from the dent, FACT: response below 3 SE), test whether reference near-receiver changes are receiver-uniform. A uniform common factor matching (ρ'/ρ)(S/S') falsifies defect (ii); per-receiver changes within noise of 0 confirm it.

### Operator form: rank R (initial hypothesis R = 8) — **UNRESOLVED**
- **Defect:** Three defects. (i) No evidence supports R = 8 per pair: the only existing rank evidence is for global bases, where WL74 needed global rank 16 for the oracle to meet the WL59 gates (FACT). WL77A's pair rank ~4 is an SVD point statistic and non-evidence. (ii) At R ≥ 13 pair factors exceed the dense block. At R ≥ 16 the factorisation is unconstrained (DERIVATION). (iii) Per-pair rank should depend on distance and context (near/edge high, far diffusive ~1), so a single R is mis-specified (DERIVATION from E1/E2).
- **Argument / counterexample:** Nonnegative rank of any 16x64 block is ≤ 16 (DERIVATION). The SVD rank is only a lower bound on nonnegative rank. Monte Carlo reference noise inflates measured rank, so any measurement needs a replica noise floor.
- **Modification:** No value adopted. If a successor uses pair factors, R must be a declared function of the pair class (near / edge-corner / far), fixed from a non-learned nonnegative-rank analysis before any training, with the noise floor stated.
- **Why it addresses the defect:** Replaces an unsupported scalar hypothesis with a quantity that has to be measured. A rank that cannot compress (≥ 13) is then reported as such rather than presented as a low-rank prior.
- **New assumptions:** Archived references are converged enough per pair for a rank estimate against the replica noise floor.
- **New cost:** A non-learned NMF analysis over about 3x10^5 pair blocks: CPU or GPU minutes (INFERENCE). Unmeasured.
- **New risks:** Rank estimates on noisy Monte Carlo blocks are biased upward. Per-class R adds a declared hyperparameter that could be tuned post hoc if not frozen first.
- **Expected effect on source generalisation:** None directly. Rank sets fit capacity and cost, not identification (WL75: rank-16 training-only benefit, FACT).
- **Falsifying experiment:** Not authorised and not recommended. Per-pair nonnegative matrix factorisation of archived reference K blocks at the target relative error, by pair class, against replica noise. R_needed ≤ 8 for every class would confirm the initial hypothesis. R_needed ≥ 13 for near or edge pairs falsifies the compressive pair-factor premise.

## 3. Mathematical and physical validity of the corrected operator C2

Holds (DERIVATION):
- **Linearity.** It is linear in the incident field, as long as z depends on geometry only.
- **Positivity.** It holds.
- **Passivity.** Σ_{j,o} K = ω·d·S / max(1, S) ≤ ω·d. With the ballistic part B, β + ω(1−β) < 1. So the ℓ1-induced (power) norm is below 1 and the coupled solve converges against a passive C.
- **Dimensions.** K is a power fraction, since m(j,o) appears in the numerator and the measure m is in S. This is consistent.
- **Entrywise locality** while S ≤ 1. It holds.

**Error E-a: the reciprocity claim is wrong as stated (DERIVATION).**
- With K a power fraction, reciprocity is m(i,a)·K(ia→jo) = m(j,ō)·K(jō→iā).
- Substituting K = d·ω·m(j,o)·z gives d(i,a)·z(ia,jo) = d(j,ō)·z(jō,iā).
- So "reciprocal z" is not sufficient. z must satisfy z_ij = d_j·s_ij with s symmetric.
- This is the same structure as the revision's own E1 form, which carries (m d)_i (m d)_j, and the same structure as its own condition on ρ (ρ ∝ S/(m d)). The C2 property list contradicts the revision's own derivation.
- Separately, exact reciprocity is not defined entrywise across the 16-incoming / 64-outgoing bin mismatch. It needs a declared restriction or prolongation between the bin sets, and the revision mentions this only as "bin nesting INFERENCE".

**Error E-b: B/K budget consistency is not specified (INFERENCE).**
- If d comes from deterministic chords while B stays on the existing Monte Carlo β quadrature, then β_B + d ≠ 1 per cell. The passivity bound then refers to a different β than the ballistic term actually uses.
- WL76 explicitly anchored to β_B.
- C1 should require B and d to be computed from the same quadrature.

**Minor validity notes:**
- χ_D is identically 1 on the single connected slab. It is harmless but adds nothing here.
- max(1, ·) has the form of a clamp. The revision correctly requires it to be predeclared and its binding fraction reported.
- The corridor width sqrt(r·ℓ) is unjustified. For a weakly absorbing slab the diffusive "banana" fills the thickness after a few mm, so it is set by H and κ rather than ℓ (INFERENCE). It is also unspecified which geometry features the corridor contains.

## 4. Is the revised architecture materially better for unseen-source transfer?

**No, and the revision does not claim it is.** It says every correction is about validity and adds no identification. That is honest, not a move to make the proposal harder to falsify.

**Caveat on the bump construction (DERIVATION).**
- The orbit-consistent bump argument is generic. It applies to any learned operator with finite supervision, including ATO and the FIA M term. On its own it does not single out BGC.
- What does discriminate is the absence of a structural mechanism that shares information across sources. R-a has one: a source-independent Q evaluated at collision points that supervised sources also visit. R-b has one too: reciprocity. BGC has none.
- The rejection therefore rests on three things together: the bump construction, the absence of cross-source sharing, and A3 targeting a factor the record calls non-binding (WL75 l.308).
- The revision's section 5 / point 5 says this in substance. The "Central mechanism sound: NO" paragraph should state it explicitly.

**Correction to Adversary 2 (DERIVATION).** Adversary 2 says reciprocity cannot separate θ from θ′ because "the modified entries pair only with unsupervised columns". That is wrong in general.
- For an unseen source i, the entries K(i→j) where j is a supervised source position (or one of its orbit images) are pinned by the supervised column j→i.
- These pinned entries are mostly far-field, so the near-field O(1) freedom survives.
- The revision's "mainly in the far field" statement is the correct version.

## 5. New limitations introduced by the corrections

1. **max(1, ·) projection.** It is non-smooth at S = 1. Wherever it binds, cross-receiver coupling and non-reciprocity return, so the binding fraction becomes a required metric.
2. **Corridor descriptor.** It is unspecified, raises input dimension and so memorisation capacity, and costs about 10^7 queries per rebuild (unmeasured).
3. **Clipping at r_c.** It can hide a second edge, as in TBE-far.
4. **Geometric frame.** It is undefined at D4-symmetric lattice points, and the tie-break there could reintroduce identity.
5. **D4h augmentation.** It risks orbit over-weighting, in line with the 2-18 % original-orientation cost in WL80.
6. **Per-class rank R.** It adds a hyperparameter that has to be frozen before any result.
7. **Deterministic chord grid.** It has its own discretisation error, and its convergence must be declared.
8. **Smooth local/far window.** It adds a second length scale.
9. **σ_t law under deformation.** It must be declared.
10. **Selective-recompute invalidation.** It still widens to the edit region dilated by the support radius, about 13-24 mm.

## Verdict: BGC REJECTED

The revision's rejection stands on verified facts and correct derivations. Before it is cited, it needs these amendments:
